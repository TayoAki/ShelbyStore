Research date: 2026-11-16. Latest full fiscal year for most companies is calendar 2025 (Walmart FY2026 ended Jan 31, 2026; Alibaba FY2026 ended Mar 31, 2026). Labels used below:
- **[CO]** = company-reported / audited (10-K, 20-F, earnings release, IPO prospectus, official statistics)

## Q1. Who are the largest ecommerce companies globally and in the US by GMV or ecommerce revenue?

### Takeaway
By GMV, the Chinese marketplaces (Alibaba's Taobao/Tmall, PDD's Pinduoduo) and Amazon (~$830B GMV in 2025, est.) are in a league of their own. Below them sit JD.com, Shopify's merchant network ($378B GMV, 2025), and Walmart (~$150B ecommerce sales, FY2026). TikTok Shop ($64B GMV est., 2025) is the fastest-growing major platform. In the US, Amazon holds roughly 36–40% of ecommerce, about 4–6x Walmart's share.

### Inferences
- Ranked by GMV, the likely 2025 order is Alibaba > Pinduoduo (PDD) ≈ Amazon (~$830B) > JD.com > Shopify merchants ($378B) > Walmart (~$150–185B) > eBay (~$80B) ≈ MercadoLibre ($65B) ≈ TikTok Shop ($64B) > Temu (~$55–70B est.) > Shein (~$42B revenue) > Coupang (~$35B revenue). The positions of Alibaba, PDD and JD rest on scale inferred from revenue and past disclosures, because none of them publishes current GMV.
- Shopify's GMV is not a retailer's sales. It sums millions of independent merchant storefronts, which makes it the main "DTC brand" aggregate and the most relevant benchmark for brand-owned ecommerce.
- In the US, Amazon's ~$440B GMV is roughly 2.5–3x Walmart US ecommerce (~$100B FY2026 1P+marketplace sales, or more on a GMV basis), and ~29x TikTok Shop US.
- Walmart is the fastest-growing large US ecommerce player in absolute dollars (~+$20B US ecom in one year). Amazon's retail growth has slowed to high single digits, with growth now coming from 3P services, ads and AWS.

## Q2. What do they sell? Category mix of the largest players

### Takeaway
Category mix splits by business model. Amazon and Walmart win on everyday essentials (health/household, home, grocery) plus electronics. Shein is mostly apparel, but non-apparel is growing. TikTok Shop is led by beauty & personal care and fashion. eBay has pivoted to collectibles, auto parts, pre-owned/luxury fashion and refurbished goods. JD is anchored in electronics/appliances but grows faster in general merchandise and grocery. Most giants report only fee or segment revenue, not GMV by category, so category data is mostly third-party estimates.

### Inferences
- The two largest "what sells" engines in Western ecommerce are (1) high-frequency consumables (health, household, beauty, grocery), where Amazon and Walmart win on speed and price, and (2) discretionary fashion and beauty, where Shein, TikTok Shop and DTC brands on Shopify win on novelty, content and price.
- Beauty & personal care punches far above its weight on social commerce (~22% of TikTok Shop US vs a much smaller share of total US ecommerce). It is demo-friendly, impulse-priced and repeat-purchase.
- Collectibles and recommerce are a defensible niche away from the Amazon/Temu price war. eBay's growth is concentrated there.

### Gaps
- No company-reported category GMV split exists for Amazon, Walmart, Alibaba, PDD/Temu, MercadoLibre or Coupang. The third-party Amazon category estimates (ECDB vs SmartScout) conflict and use different taxonomies.
- No reliable Temu category mix was found.
- Walmart does not disclose grocery's share of ecommerce sales.
- Shein's women's-apparel-specific share was not found.

## Q3. Business models and growth trajectories (2023–2026), including Temu/Shein, TikTok Shop, and de minimis/tariffs

### Takeaway
The 2023–2024 boom of Chinese cross-border, ship-direct models (Temu, Shein) ran into the US de minimis repeal in 2025. That flattened Temu US, slowed Shein to single-digit growth with lower profits, and cut Shein's IPO valuation to ~$26.5B. Over the same period, TikTok Shop (social/live commerce) roughly doubled GMV each year, and incumbents grew steadily: Amazon ~9–12%, Walmart ecommerce ~20–28%, Shopify ~29%, MercadoLibre ~26%. The shared structural shift is toward third-party marketplaces plus advertising, which are higher-margin than first-party retail.

### Cited Findings

**Business-model shift toward marketplaces/3P**
- Amazon sellers' share of GMV rose from 60% (2019) to 69% (2025). The marketplace accounted for ~76% of Amazon's ~$495B GMV increase since 2019. Seller share gained only ~20 bps in 2025, versus ~150 bps/year over the prior decade [EST] — [Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazon-gmv-surpassed-800-billion-in-2025)
- Amazon GMV growth has run 6–10% annually since 2022, versus ~46% in 2020 [EST] — [Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazon-gmv-surpassed-800-billion-in-2025)
- Shein's marketplace/service revenue rose from 2.7% of revenue (2023) to 14.3% (Q1 2026) [CO] — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/shein-business-model-into-fashions-top-15-at-a-70-discount/2026082990051)
- Walmart US marketplace grew ~17% in FY2026 quarters, helped by more sellers using Walmart Fulfillment Services [CO/SEC] — [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2025/11/21/walmart-online-sales-q3-fy26/)
- eBay's 2025 advertising revenue ~$2B [CO/SEC] — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/ebay-announces-strong-2025-results-and-definitive-agreement-to-acquire-depop/2026021986365)

### Inferences
- The end of de minimis took away the duty-free, direct-from-factory parcel cost advantage. That pushed Temu and Shein toward local-warehouse and marketplace models that look more like Amazon's, and it narrowed the price gap to domestic sellers. Amazon Haul shows the incumbents' counter-move.
- Of the cross-border players, TikTok Shop was least affected, because most US GMV ships from US-based sellers. Its growth stems from content-driven discovery rather than parcel economics.
- Growth is decelerating for the "hypergrowth" Chinese cross-border players (Temu, Shein) and accelerating for platforms tied to content (TikTok Shop) or omnichannel speed (Walmart).

## Q4. Largest ecommerce product categories overall (US and global)

### Takeaway
Globally, fashion (~27% of ecommerce revenue) and electronics (~24%) are the two largest online categories by ECDB's estimates. China accounts for roughly half of all global ecommerce, and there online food sales are growing fastest. In the US, ecommerce is ~17% of retail (Census, Q2 2026) and ~$1.23T (2025, Digital Commerce 360). Growth is now led by mass merchants, food & beverage, and health & beauty. Authoritative US category share tables (eMarketer, Statista) are paywalled.

### Cited Findings

**Global size**
- eMarketer: 2025 worldwide retail ecommerce ~$6.419T, 20.5% of global retail. Over 80% of 2025 ecommerce happens in China, the US and Western Europe. Ex-China, ecommerce share of retail falls to ~12.8% [EST] — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/ecommerce-account-more-than-20--of-worldwide-retail-sales-despite-slowdown); [eMarketer chart](https://www.emarketer.com/chart/270797/Over-80-of-2025s-Worldwide-Ecommerce-Sales-Will-Happen-China-US-Western-Europe-of-worldwide-retail-ecommerce-sales-select-countriesregi)
- Other trackers put 2025 global retail ecommerce at $6.4–6.9T. ECDB's narrower definition gives ~$4.89T [EST] — [Ecommerce Germany](https://ecommercegermany.com/blog/e-commerce-stats/)

**Global category mix**
- Fashion is the largest online category at ~27.3–27.4% of global ecommerce revenue (~$1,307B in 2025). Electronics is second at ~24.0% (~$1,131B) [EST – ECDB] — [ECDB global fashion](https://www.ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/market/ww/fashion); [ECDB global electronics](https://ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/market/ww/electronics); [ECDB category mix](https://ecdb.com/blog/leading-ecommerce-categories-in-top-markets/5134)
- Online penetration within category (2026): fashion ~38.8% online, electronics ~45.7% online [EST – ECDB] — [Ecommerce Germany](https://ecommercegermany.com/blog/e-commerce-hits-25-of-global-retail-as-categories-diverge/)

**China (largest market)**
- 2025 online retail sales RMB 15,972.2B (+8.6%). Physical goods online RMB 13,092.3B (+5.2%), equal to 26.1% of total retail sales of consumer goods. By category, food +14.5%, clothing +1.9%, daily-use goods +4.1% [CO – National Bureau of Statistics] — [NBS December 2025 release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260120_1962354.html); [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.us/why-headline-growth-hasnt-translated-into-a-broad-retail-rebound-in-china/)

**United States**
- Census Q2 2026: seasonally adjusted ecommerce $340.2B, 17.1% of total retail (16.4% unadjusted), +12.2% y/y vs total retail +6.7%. Q3 2026 data is due Nov 19, 2026 [CO – US Census] — [Census Quarterly E-Commerce](https://www.census.gov/retail/eCommerce.html); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/commerce/ecommerce/2026/ecommerce-takes-17-1percent-of-retail-sales-as-online-growth-accelerates)
- Census share trend (SA): Q2 2025 16.3%, Q3 2025 16.4%, Q4 2025 16.7%, Q1 2026 17.0%, Q2 2026 17.1% [CO] — [FRED ECOMPCTSA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=Bebu)
- Digital Commerce 360: US ecommerce $1.234T in 2025 (+5.4%), up from $1.170T in 2024. That is 23.1% of "total sales" on D360's methodology (which excludes autos, fuel, etc.) vs 16.4% on the Commerce Department basis. Fastest-growing categories in 2025: Mass Merchants +14.4%, Food & Beverage +13% (to $41.58B online), Health & Beauty +11% [EST] — [Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/article/us-ecommerce-sales/)
- eMarketer's US category share forecast exists but is paywalled. eMarketer's "household & personal consumables" bucket includes OTC medicine, vitamins, personal care and beauty — [eMarketer forecast](https://www.emarketer.com/forecasts/584b26021403070290f93a2d/5851918a0626310a2c186abd/)
- Long-run growth forecast (2017–2028 CAGR) for US online sales by category: beauty/health/personal & household care 14.9%, fashion 11.6%, electronics 6.4% [EST] — [SOAX](https://soax.com/research/us-ecommerce-market-size)
- Census Annual Retail Trade Survey (which published merchandise-line ecommerce detail) was discontinued after the 2022 reference year and folded into the Annual Integrated Economic Survey. Electronic shopping and mail-order houses (NAICS 4541) sales were $1,117.0B in 2022 [CO] — [Census ARTS](https://www.census.gov/arts)

### Inferences
- Globally and in the US, the biggest online categories by dollars are fashion/apparel and electronics. The fastest-growing are consumables (food & beverage, health & beauty, household essentials). That matches Amazon's report that "everyday essentials" are ~1/3 of US units and growing ~2x as fast.
- China's online food growth (+14.5%) far outpacing clothing (+1.9%) mirrors the US pattern: grocery and consumables are the incremental growth frontier, and apparel is maturing.

### Gaps
- **US share of ecommerce by category (latest):** eMarketer and Statista tables are paywalled, and Digital Commerce 360's 14-category breakdown is in a paid report. My background knowledge is that eMarketer ranks computer & consumer electronics and apparel & accessories as the two largest US online categories (each roughly one-fifth of sales), followed by health/personal care/beauty, furniture/home, toys/hobby and food & beverage. **This was not verified with a source in this session and should not be cited without confirmation.**
- No government source gives a current (2023+) US ecommerce-by-merchandise-line breakdown, because ARTS was discontinued after 2022.
- ECDB's global category shares come from a single vendor with a narrower market definition and should be treated as indicative only.
