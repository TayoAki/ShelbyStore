# Most successful digitally-native consumable brands: food, beverage, snacks, meal kits, pets

Research date: 2026-11-16. Figures are labeled by year and evidence grade:
- **[FILED]**: audited or regulator-filed (SEC 8-K/10-K, listed-company results, UK Companies House accounts)
- **[ACQUIRER]**: price disclosed by the buyer in its own press release or filing
- **[CO]**: company-reported (founder, spokesperson or press release) but unaudited, since the company is private
- **[EST]**: third-party estimate (PitchBook, Sacra, eightx, Dealroom, Particl, newsletters)
- **[UNVERIFIED]**: seen only in a search-result summary or a weak or secondary source, and not confirmed on a fetched page

Note on method: the web-search budget ran out partway through. Some items (Ghost/KDP, BARK take-private details, Daily Harvest illness counts, Wild One, Fable Pets) could not be researched and are listed as gaps.

## Q1. Which brands have the strongest evidence of success (revenue, growth, profitability, valuation, acquisition price)?

### Takeaway
The strongest hard evidence sits with four groups. The first is Chewy, which reports audited numbers. The second is DTC/social-born brands that sold at a disclosed price: Poppi to PepsiCo for $1.95B gross, Alani Nu to Celsius for $1.8B gross, Huel to Danone for about €1bn as reported by the FT, Ollie to Agrolimen for over $600M as reported by Reuters, and Nom Nom to Mars for about $1B. The third is the large private brands with company-reported revenue and profitability: Olipop ($400M in 2024, profitable), Thrive Market ($700M+ in sales), and The Farmer's Dog (about $1.2B annualized, PitchBook estimate). The fourth is the venture-scale beverage brands: Liquid Death ($1.4B valuation, estimated revenue only) and Athletic Brewing (>$130M in 2024 sales). Smaller brands such as Magic Spoon, Graza, Fly By Jing, Mid-Day Squares, Native Pet and Super Coffee have retail-door evidence but little verifiable revenue.

## Q2. What does each sell, at what price, and what share of sales is DTC vs retail? (DTC-first to retail pattern)

### Takeaway
Almost every successful food and beverage brand here started online, with DTC, Amazon or social, and then made most of its sales in physical retail. Beverage especially scales through grocery, club and convenience stores. The main exceptions are subscription businesses whose product needs cold-chain or personalization: The Farmer's Dog (DTC-only), Ollie, Thrive Market and the meal kits. Few brands disclose a precise DTC/retail split. The available splits are estimates.

### Inferences
- **The pattern:**
  1. The brand uses DTC, Amazon or social to prove demand and build a story.
  2. It moves into natural or premium grocery (Whole Foods, Sprouts).
  3. It moves into mass and club (Target, Walmart, Costco, Kroger).
  4. It sells to a strategic acquirer.
  Poppi, Liquid Death, Magic Spoon, Graza, Daily Harvest and Native Pet all follow some version of this.
- **Shelf-stable, impulse or low-ticket consumables** (soda, water, cereal, snacks) cannot sustain DTC unit economics on shipping cost. Retail becomes the majority channel, roughly 60–80%+ where estimates exist (Graza, Super Coffee).
- **High-ticket, perishable or personalized consumables** stay DTC-subscription: fresh dog food ($2–30/day), meal kits (about €67 AOV) and membership grocery ($60/yr). There, DTC shipping cost is absorbed by a larger basket and repeat frequency.
- **BARK is a cautionary data point.** Its DTC segment shrank while its retail (commerce) segment grew. Retail diversification became the hedge against paid-acquisition-dependent subscriptions.

- Product shelf prices for Olipop, Poppi, Magic Spoon and Huel were not captured in fetched sources.

## Q3. How do subscription/autoship economics work in pets and consumables?

### Takeaway
Chewy's audited figures show Autoship customers generating 83.3% of FY2025 net sales ($10.5B), up from 79.2%. Recurring replenishment of food and medicine is the core of the profitable pet e-commerce model, though adjusted EBITDA margins are thin at 5.7%. The DTC subscription brands show the other side. Without heavy paid acquisition, subscription bases shrink: BARK cut marketing 41% and DTC revenue fell 25%. Mature subscription businesses like HelloFresh improve margins by shrinking to their loyal core.

### Cited Findings
- **Chewy Autoship**: **[FILED]** — [Chewy Q4 FY2025 Exhibit 99.1](https://s23.q4cdn.com/610444331/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/CHWY-Q4-2025-Exhibit-99-1-Filing-Day-Final.pdf)
  - FY2025 Autoship customer sales were $10,497.1M, or 83.3% of net sales, up 11.8%. FY2024 was $9,393.3M, or 79.2%.
  - Q4 FY2025 Autoship was 84.0% of net sales, versus 80.6% a year earlier.
- **Definition caveat**: An "Autoship customer" is one with at least one Autoship shipment in the past 364 days. "Autoship customer sales" therefore include non-Autoship orders by those customers. **[FILED]** — [Chewy Exhibit 99.1](https://s23.q4cdn.com/610444331/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/CHWY-Q4-2025-Exhibit-99-1-Filing-Day-Final.pdf)
- **Chewy margin profile, FY2025**: Gross margin was 29.8%, adjusted EBITDA margin 5.7%, and GAAP net margin 1.8%. There were 21.3M active customers spending $591 each a year. **[FILED]** — [Chewy Exhibit 99.1](https://s23.q4cdn.com/610444331/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/CHWY-Q4-2025-Exhibit-99-1-Filing-Day-Final.pdf)
- **The Farmer's Dog subscription**: Frozen deliveries arrive every 7–60 days depending on dog size, choosing from five recipes. Pricing scales with dog size, from about $2/day to $20–30/day. New customers get a 50% intro discount. — [Contrary](https://research.contrary.com/company/the-farmers-dog)
- **BARK DTC subscription, Q3 FY2026 (quarter to Dec 31, 2025)**: **[FILED]** — [BARK 8-K Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819574/000162828026005773/exhibit991-q32026pressrele.htm)
  - DTC orders were 2.427M, down from 3.332M, with an AOV of $31.41.
  - DTC gross margin was 69.2% excluding BARK Air.
  - Advertising and marketing fell 41.3% to $16.1M, and CAC was the "lowest in nearly three years." DTC revenue fell 25.0% because the company "carried fewer subscriptions into the quarter."
  - DTC consumables revenue was $30.2M, down from $39.8M, and toys and accessories were $46.0M, down from $64.3M.
- **BARK on retention**: The CEO said BARK "stop[ped] spending on subscribers we couldn't retain profitably." In FY2026 it is exiting its kibble and toppers food lines to focus on toys, treats and experiences. — [Global Pet Industry](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/bark-prioritizes-profitability-over-subscriber-growth/)
- **HelloFresh, FY2025**: **[FILED]** — [HelloFresh FY2025 release](https://www.finanzen.at/nachrichten/aktien/eqs-news-fy-2025-hellofresh-se-continues-to-show-strong-aebitda-performance-while-efficiency-program-progresses-meaningfully-1035940089)
  - Orders fell 12.3% to 100.53M, and AOV was about flat at €66.8.
  - The meal-kit AEBITDA margin nonetheless rose to 13.5%, from 9.8%.
  - RTE (Factor) margin went negative at -1.2%. The company cited "regulatory driven operational and manufacturing bottlenecks" in the US that hurt retention.
  - 2026 guidance: revenue down 3–6% in constant currency, AEBITDA of €375–425M, and an exit from Italy and Spain.
- **Thrive Market membership**: 1.6M paid members at $60/yr on $700M+ in sales (2025). **[CO]** — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/03/05/thrive-markets-millennial-ceo-wellness-influencers-700-million-funding/)

### Inferences
- Thrive Market's figures imply about $96M a year in membership-fee revenue (1.6M × $60) and roughly $440 in sales per member a year. This is my calculation from company-reported figures. The fee acts as a margin cushion similar to Costco's.
- **Chewy vs. BARK** is the clearest contrast. Chewy's autoship covers necessity consumables (food, litter, Rx), with a basket of $591/yr per customer that keeps growing. BARK's subscription covers discretionary toys and treats at an AOV of about $31, and the base shrinks as soon as acquisition spend stops. Necessity-plus-replenishment subscriptions are durable; novelty "box" subscriptions are not.
- HelloFresh shows that mature meal-kit subscription businesses can be profitable at the segment level (13.5% AEBITDA). They get there by shrinking to loyal customers rather than growing.

### Gaps
- No public churn or retention, CAC or LTV figures were found for The Farmer's Dog, Ollie, Huel subscriptions or Chewy Autoship. Chewy does not disclose Autoship retention.
- Chewy+ paid-membership counts were not in the press release.
- Huel's subscription share and discount mechanics were not found.
- Factor's standalone revenue is not disclosed.

## Q4. Which brands stumbled (Daily Harvest, Blue Apron, BARK, others) and why?

### Takeaway
Failures cluster in three places. The first is pandemic-inflated meal delivery: Blue Apron, Freshly and Daily Harvest all sold or shut at a small fraction of peak value. The second is acquisition-dependent novelty subscriptions: BARK's revenue fell 18.5% in FY2026 with continued losses. The third is hype-driven creator brands: Prime's sales collapsed after a roughly $1.2B 2023 peak. Food-safety crises (Daily Harvest) and operational bottlenecks (HelloFresh's US RTE business) compounded the demand problems.

### Cited Findings
- **Blue Apron (meal kits)**:
  - Sold to Wonder Group (Marc Lore) for $13/share, about $103M in equity value. The deal closed November 2023.
  - Before the deal it warned of "substantial doubt" about continuing as a going concern.
  - 2022 net loss was $109.7M on $458.5M revenue (down 3%).
  - It had earlier sold its fulfillment infrastructure to FreshRealm for $50M.
  - **[FILED, as reported]** — [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/blue-apron-sells-loss-making-us-meal-kit-business-to-wonder-group/); [Food Manufacturing](https://www.foodmanufacturing.com/consumer-trends/news/22874608/meal-kit-company-blue-apron-acquired-for-103m); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/11/13/mark-lore-wonder-group-blue-apron-deal-meal-delivery/)
  - Reported as about 95% below its roughly $1.89B 2017 IPO valuation **[secondary; exact outlet attribution from search summary]**. — [Sherwood](https://www.sherwood.news/business/blue-apron-business-model-loss)
- **Freshly (prepared meals DTC)**:
  - Nestlé bought it in 2020 for $950M plus up to $550M in earnouts.
  - In November 2022, Nestlé moved it into a JV with L Catterton (59% L Catterton, 41% Nestlé) targeting foodservice.
  - Home delivery ended in late December 2022.
  - Nestlé's CEO said it "had not met expectations," citing changes in consumer tracking and post-COVID behavior.
  - Former shareholders sued Nestlé in May 2023 over unpaid earnouts.
  - Source: [Food Processing](https://www.foodprocessing.com/business-of-food-beverage/business-strategies/news/21529171/nestle-ends-freshlys-home-meal-delivery-service); [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/nestle-spins-off-freshly-into-l-catterton-venture-updates-financial-targets/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/17/freshly-shareholders-including-insight-partners-sue-nestle-for-failing-to-make-earnout-payments)
- **Daily Harvest (frozen plant-based DTC)**:
  - Valued at $1.1B in 2021, per a Bloomberg report, and raised $133M+.
  - In 2022 it withdrew its Crumbles product over tara flour, which was linked to severe gallbladder problems in "hundreds of consumers." The FDA later deemed tara flour unsafe.
  - It settled a class action for $23M in 2023 without admitting wrongdoing, and two suppliers paid a further $7.7M in 2024.
  - Founder-CEO Rachel Drori stepped down in 2023.
  - It launched GLP-1 companion meals in 2024 in response to "relatively slow" sales.
  - Chobani acquired it in May 2025 for an undisclosed price.
  - Source: [Green Queen](https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/chobani-daily-harvest-vegan-smoothies-acquisition/); [Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/chobani-acquires-daily-harvest/)
  - Exact illness counts were not verified.
- **BARK (BarkBox, Super Chewer, BARK Air)**:
  - FY2026 revenue was $394.8M, down 18.5%, with a net loss of $39.0M (versus $32.9M) and adjusted EBITDA of $0.2M (versus $5.4M).
  - Q4 revenue was $86.6M, down 25%.
  - FY2027 guidance is $325–340M in revenue and $7–10M in adjusted EBITDA.
  - It is exiting kibble and toppers.
  - **[FILED, as reported]** — [Global Pet Industry](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/bark-prioritizes-profitability-over-subscriber-growth/)
  - In Q3 FY2026 it held $21.7M in cash, repaid its $45M convertible notes, and gave no Q4 guidance "due to a Special Committee review of non-binding proposal letters," indicating take-private or acquisition interest. — [BARK 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819574/000162828026005773/exhibit991-q32026pressrele.htm)
  - A $40M buyback was announced with the FY results **[UNVERIFIED, search summary]**. — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260609274754/en/BARK-Reports-Fiscal-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2026-Results)
- **Prime Hydration (creator-led, Logan Paul and KSI, with Congo Brands)**:
  - Bloomberg reported in November 2023 that Prime was "set to" reach $1.2B in first-year sales. This was a projection, not a confirmed result.
  - US sales fell 42% year over year, per Circana data posted by Darren Rovell (July 2025).
  - Problems cited: PFAS lawsuits, a breach-of-contract suit, FDA scrutiny of kid-targeted marketing, and a "boom-or-bust" viral-stunt strategy.
  - Source: [Tubefilter, Jul 22 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/22/prime-hydration-sales-decline-year-over-year-creator-product/)
  - UK turnover reportedly fell from £112.2m (2023) to £32.8m (2024), per Companies House. **[UNVERIFIED, search summary]** — [City A.M.](https://www.cityam.com/?p=2342008)

  - A repeat-purchase rate of about 12% has been cited **[UNVERIFIED]**. — [The Robin Report](https://therobinreport.com/?p=98106)
- **HelloFresh/Factor**: Revenue fell 9% in constant currency in 2025, orders fell 12.3%, and the RTE margin turned negative. The company is exiting Italy and Spain. **[FILED]** — [HelloFresh FY2025 release](https://www.finanzen.at/nachrichten/aktien/eqs-news-fy-2025-hellofresh-se-continues-to-show-strong-aebitda-performance-while-efficiency-program-progresses-meaningfully-1035940089)
- **Liquid Death (slowing, not failed)**: Estimated growth fell from about 139% (2023) to 27% (2024), with roughly flat about $340M projected for 2025. **[EST]** — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/liquid-death/)

### Inferences
- **Common failure drivers:**
  - **Pandemic over-extrapolation.** Freshly ($950M in 2020) and Daily Harvest ($1.1B in 2021) were priced on 2020–21 demand that did not persist.
  - **Paid-acquisition dependence.** BARK and Blue Apron shrank as soon as marketing was cut, which exposed weak organic retention.
  - **Product-safety or operational failures** in perishable food: the Daily Harvest recall and HelloFresh's US RTE bottlenecks.
  - **Hype without repeat purchase.** Prime peaked on viral stunts but showed weak repeat purchasing.
- **Shared traits of the survivors:**
  - They moved into retail early (Poppi, Olipop, Liquid Death).
  - Or they sell a high-frequency necessity on subscription (Chewy, The Farmer's Dog).
  - And they reached profitability before or around exit (Olipop 2024, Huel, Mid-Day Squares 2024, Thrive 2023).

- I could not verify BARK's take-private bidder or terms, or any NYSE listing-compliance history, because the search budget ran out.
- Daily Harvest's exact illness count, layoffs and the Chobani price are undisclosed or unverified.
- Prime's global revenue for 2024 and 2025 is unverified, since the company is private.
- Blue Apron's post-acquisition performance under Wonder is unknown.
