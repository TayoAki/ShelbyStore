Research date: 2026-11-16. Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[AUDITED/FILING]**: audited statements, SEC/SZSE filings, or the company's formal earnings release.
- **[COMPANY-STATED]**: a company claim in a press release, funding announcement, or interview. Not audited.
- **[3P-EST]**: a third-party estimate (Sacra, ECDB, Grips, EchoTik, Pitchbook, etc.).
- **[SECONDARY]**: a news or aggregator report of a filing that I could not open directly. Treat it as likely accurate but unverified.
- **[UNVERIFIED]**: a single source, an unclear date, or conflicting reports.

Currency conversions (RMB to USD) are my own approximations at about 7.2 RMB/USD and are labeled "approx."

Note on method: the web-search budget ran out before every gap was closed (Tonal 2025 layoffs, Nomad, dbrand and SharkNinja DTC share were left open). **Nomad and dbrand are dropped** because I found no substantiating data.

## Q1. Which brands have the strongest evidence of success (revenue, growth, profitability, valuation, IPO, acquisition)?

### Takeaway
The strongest verified winners split into two groups. The first is **Amazon-native Chinese accessory makers**: Anker made about RMB 30.5B (about US$4.2B) of 2025 revenue with RMB 2.55B profit, and Ugreen made RMB 9.49B with RMB 705M profit (audited). The second is **hardware-plus-subscription health wearables**. Oura filed its S-1 with $907.9M of FY2025 revenue, up 123%, and is GAAP-profitable at the net-income line. Whoop is valued at $10.1B on a stated $1.1B bookings run rate. SharkNinja ($6.4B sales) is the scale benchmark, but it is retail-led rather than ecommerce-native. Peloton went from a ~$49B pandemic peak to a ~$2.4B-revenue company that only reached its first full-year GAAP profit in FY2026, with subscribers still falling.

### Inferences
- **Two playbooks dominate the verifiable winners.** (a) Amazon-first, high-SKU, low-to-mid-price accessories from Shenzhen (Anker, Ugreen): large, profitable and publicly audited. (b) Premium health wearables whose membership and data create recurring revenue (Oura, Whoop, and on a smaller scale Eight Sleep and Owlet): high-growth, now at or near profitability, with IPOs filed or announced.
- The best-evidenced US/EU digitally native brands are **wearables**, not accessories. Accessory DTC brands (Peak Design, Bellroy, Casetify, Nomad, dbrand, Away) are mostly private and publish little. Their success claims are older or estimated, so they rank below the audited Chinese accessory firms.
- Oura is now the strongest "DTC-born" success in the set by audited numbers: about $1.6B+ trailing revenue (my estimate from the 9M figure plus the prior-year Q4), +74% growth and positive net income. It is also shifting toward wholesale.
- Whoop's $1.1B is a bookings run rate, not GAAP revenue, so it is not directly comparable to Oura's S-1 revenue.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 audited revenue for Therabody, Hyperice, Away, Peak Design, Bellroy, Casetify, Nomad or dbrand. Nomad and dbrand were dropped.
- Ring's standalone revenue is not disclosed by Amazon.
- Eight Sleep's actual annual revenue is not disclosed; only cumulative figures and growth multiples are available.
- I could not open Anker's official 2025 annual report on cninfo directly; the figures come from consistent secondary summaries.

## Q2. What does each sell, at what price, with what business model (hardware + subscription), and through which channels?

### Takeaway
Subscription-attached hardware is the dominant model among the winners, but the subscription share varies widely. It is about 85% for Whoop (Sacra estimate), 67% for Peloton (audited) and about 20% for Oura (S-1). Accessory leaders (Anker, Ugreen) sell no subscriptions at all. Channel strategy is moving toward multichannel. Oura's DTC share fell from about 73% to about 60% as retail grew, and Anker's offline share is now about 30%.

## Q3. What role do Amazon and TikTok Shop play for electronics/accessory brands (Anker as the archetype)?

### Takeaway
Amazon is the launchpad and still the single largest channel for the Chinese accessory leaders. Anker's Amazon dependence fell from about 80% of revenue (2016) to 54% (2021) and about 50% (H1 2025), while its own websites grew fastest (+43%). Chinese sellers now dominate the long tail of Amazon electronics. TikTok Shop is a fast-growing, creator-driven secondary channel in which electronics is a top-five US category, but brand-level electronics GMV data is sparse and comes only from third-party trackers.

### Cited Findings
- **Anker's Amazon trajectory:** 80% of revenue from Amazon in 2016 → 54% in 2021. In 2021 Amazon sales were $1.03B of $1.89B total, with $685M from brick-and-mortar and $168M from other marketplaces and owned websites. China was about 3% of sales. Amazon-only sales grew 19% against 34% overall. "Remarkably few sellers ever achieved $1 billion in sales" — [Marketplace Pulse, Jun 7, 2022](https://marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazon-native-brand-anker-reaches-1-billion-sales) [SECONDARY of Anker filings].
- **H1 2025:** Amazon was 49.97% of Anker revenue (vs 52.26% in H1 2024) and grew 27.5%. Own websites were 10.26% and grew 42.6%. Amazon "remains Anker's most important channel" — [Baijing](https://www.baijing.cn/article/53904) [SECONDARY].
- Deep News (Dec 2025): Amazon contributed "over 50% of revenue in the past three years," and the Hong Kong IPO proceeds are meant to fund social commerce, offline retail and proprietary websites to reduce Amazon dependence — [iTiger/Deep News](https://www.itiger.com/news/1141203152) [SECONDARY].
- A third-party tracker estimates Amazon at 85.6% of Anker's tracked US retail revenue across Amazon and five other US retailers (2026 YTD). This is US retail only, not company revenue — [Grips Intelligence](https://gripsintelligence.com/insights/brands/anker) [3P-EST].
- Anker's 2025 recalls of at least 2.38M power-bank units (China, Japan, US) weighed on profit growth — [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1181701403) [SECONDARY]. This shows the product-safety and reputational risk for Amazon-scale battery sellers.
- **Chinese sellers on Amazon:** 49% of top Amazon.com sellers are China-based versus 47% US-based. Chinese sellers "won the top 10,000" by count while US brands hold the top 100 — [Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/china-won-amazons-top-10000-america-kept-its-top-100), [Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/chinese-sellers-outnumber-us-sellers-on-amazoncom). In niches such as phone accessories and small electronics, Chinese sellers account for "well over 50%" of revenue — [SmartScout (citing Marketplace Pulse data)](https://www.smartscout.com/zh-cn/blog/amazon-chinese-sellers) [3P].
- **Amazon as acquirer:** Amazon bought Ring (reported >$1B, 2018) after backing it through the Alexa Fund, and bought Blink a few months earlier — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2018/02/27/amazon-buys-ring-the-smart-door-bell-maker-it-backed-through-alexa-fund.html), [Retail TouchPoints](https://retailtouchpoints.com/features/mergers-and-acquisitions/amazon-advances-into-home-security-with-1-billion-ring-acquisition).
- **Amazon as a must-have retailer even for omnichannel incumbents:** SharkNinja's Amazon, Costco and Walmart each exceed 10% of net sales (45.7% combined, 2025) — [PolicyRisk (quoting 10-K)](https://policyrisk.com/world/company/SN) [SECONDARY]. Oura's two largest retail customers are 12% and 10% of revenue, and Amazon is among its retail partners — [parameter.io (S-1 coverage)](https://parameter.io/oura-oura-files-for-nasdaq-ipo-with-1-4b-revenue-and-5m-active-users/) [SECONDARY].
- **TikTok Shop:** electronics is one of the top-selling US TikTok Shop categories, alongside womenswear, beauty and personal care, health, and sports and outdoors. US TikTok Shop sales were up 120% year on year — [Retail TouchPoints](https://www.retailtouchpoints.com/topics/customer-experience/two-years-in-tiktok-shop-is-a-commercial-powerhouse-a-look-at-the-numbers) [SECONDARY].
- Anker runs a US TikTok Shop flagship (AnkerUS, mainly power banks, power stations and accessories). One Anker power bank passed US$10M GMV on TikTok Shop (Aug 2024) — [EchoTik blog](https://echotikai.blogspot.com/2024/08/gmv-exceeds-10-million-anker-power-bank.html) [3P-EST, 2024].
- TikTok Shop GMV totals conflict by methodology: US 2025 is reported as $15.1B by [DealStreetAsia](https://www.dealstreetasia.com/?p=472662), versus $33.8B in [another aggregator](https://www.dashboardly.io/statistics/tiktok-shop-revenue-gmv-statistics) [UNVERIFIED/conflicting]. FastMoss's US 2025 recap ranks Beauty, Women's Apparel, Home and Accessories at the top and gives no electronics figure — [FastMoss](https://www.fastmoss.com/blog/u-s-tiktok-shop-2025-sales-recap-top-products-creator-trends-fastest-growing-categories/) [3P].

### Inferences
- **The Anker archetype:** launch on Amazon with engineering-led, review-driven commodity accessories, then reach about $1B on Amazon and diversify. Even after 10+ years and RMB 30B of revenue, Amazon is still about half of revenue, so "diversification" means adding channels, not leaving Amazon.
- Owned websites are the fastest-growing channel for Anker (+43% year on year), but at about 10% of revenue they remain small next to Amazon.
- For premium wearables (Oura, Whoop), Amazon and big-box retail are reach channels added after DTC proved demand. For accessory makers it is the other way round: they start on Amazon and add DTC later.
- TikTok Shop matters most for impulse-priced, demonstrable gadgets such as power banks and chargers. There is no evidence in these sources that it is a primary channel for premium wearables or fitness hardware.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 brand-level TikTok Shop GMV for Anker, Ugreen, Therabody or other electronics brands was found from a reliable source. The search budget ran out before trying EchoTik, Kalodata or Momentum Works directly.
- No current Marketplace Pulse ranking of Anker's position among the top Amazon sellers.
- Anker's full-year 2025 Amazon percentage (only H1 2025 was found).

## Q4. Which stumbled (Peloton, Mirror, Tonal) and why?

### Takeaway
Connected-fitness hardware was the biggest casualty of the pandemic demand pull-forward. Peloton fell from about $4.0B revenue and about $49B market cap (2021) to about $2.4B revenue, with eight-plus percent annual subscriber declines continuing into FY2026. Lululemon wrote off nearly all of its $500M Mirror purchase within about 2.5 years. Tonal's valuation fell from $1.6B to about $0.55–0.6B. The common causes were expensive single-purpose hardware, demand that faded after lockdowns, rising churn, and (for Mirror) failed integration.

### Cited Findings
**Peloton**
- At the peak, FY2021 revenue was about $4.0B. Market cap was about $49B (Jan 2021) — [Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/at-4-9bn-pelotons-market-cap-value-has-plunged-to-just-10-of-what-it-was-in-january-2021), [Wolf Street](https://wolfstreet.com/2023/08/23/peloton-pukes-stock-sallies-to-new-record-low-97-from-peak/) [SECONDARY].
- In Nov 2021 the stock plunged after Peloton cut its annual sales forecast, with market cap falling from about $26B to about $18B in a day — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2021/11/05/peloton-shares-down-growth/). FY2023 revenue was down 22% to $2.8B, and the stock was down about 97% from its peak by Aug 2023 — [Wolf Street](https://wolfstreet.com/2023/08/23/peloton-pukes-stock-sallies-to-new-record-low-97-from-peak/) [SECONDARY].
- FY2025: hardware revenue −17.6%, subscription revenue −2.1%, churn up to 1.6%/month — [10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1639825/000163982525000138/pton-20250630.htm) [AUDITED].
- FY2026: first annual GAAP profit ($63.2M) and $378M FCF, achieved through cost cuts and price increases rather than growth. Subscribers were −8.8%, Q4 churn spiked to 2.2% (Peloton blamed a payment-failure email change), FY2027 revenue guidance is down about 3.9%, and a $23.8M patent-litigation accrual was booked. Shares fell sharply — [PeloBuddy](https://www.pelobuddy.com/peloton-q4-fy2026-earnings/), [Insider Monkey](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/peloton-pton-turns-the-corner-but-subscribers-keep-slipping-1826877/) [SECONDARY].

**Mirror (Lululemon)**
- Lululemon acquired Mirror for $500M in June 2020. It booked a $442.7M post-tax impairment in Q4 2022 after holiday hardware sales "came in below expectations" (CFO Meghan Frank: "The overall at-home fitness space remains challenged"). The reasons cited were cooling home-fitness demand, unsuccessful integration, a user experience that consumers rated below Peloton and Tonal, and a $39/mo Lululemon Studio subscription that "didn't meet our expectations" (CEO Calvin McDonald) — [Yahoo Finance, Mar 29, 2023](https://au.finance.yahoo.com/news/lululemons-ill-timed-mirror-acquisition-is-now-almost-worthless-124822875.html) [SECONDARY of earnings release].
- About 100 Lululemon Studio employees were laid off (Jul 2023), followed by about 120 more after a content deal with Peloton. Lululemon explored selling Mirror — [Retail Dive](https://retaildive.com/news/lululemon-studio-mirror-layoffs-one-hundred/687544), [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/lululemon-layoffs-studio-division/), [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/lululemons-reported-attempts-to-sell-mirror-deals-another-blow-to-the-at-home-fitness-industry/).
- A later filing reportedly valued Mirror at about $58M, and Lululemon stopped selling Mirror hardware — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/lululemon-desperately-wants-out-of-struggling-connected-fitness-space), [SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/mirror/2219.tag) [SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED].

**Tonal** (wall-mounted digital strength trainer)
- 2021 Series E: $250M at a $1.6B valuation. In 2023 it raised $130M from existing investors at a reported $550–600M valuation (WSJ via Retail Dive; "nearly $600M" per Front Office Sports). The founder stepped down as CEO in Apr 2023 — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/tonal-raises-130m-after-big-value-drop), [Retail Dive](https://retaildive.com/news/lululemon-studio-mirror-layoffs-one-hundred/687544) [SECONDARY].
- Front Office Sports headline: Tonal to "cut 35% of workforce" (reported Feb 2025) as it pursued profitability ahead of a possible IPO — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/connected-fitness-giant-tonal-to-cut-35-of-workforce/) [SECONDARY; date per search summary, not confirmed in article]. Tonal 2 launched in Jan 2025 — [BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20250108657893/en/Tonal-Ushers-In-The-Future-Of-Fitness-With-Tonal-2-%E2%80%93-Designed-To-Improve-Performance-And-Build-Muscle-Faster).
- CEO churn: Darren MacDonald was named CEO in Sep 2024 and Todd Bartee became CEO in Feb 2026, per Tonal's site — [BusinessWire Sep 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240910974145/en), [Tonal About](https://www.tonal.com/about/) [COMPANY-STATED]. PitchBook lists about $678M raised in total, including a $20M debt deal (Mar 2026) — [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/125615-62) [3P]. No audited revenue or profitability was found.

**Other cautionary notes**
- Owlet, though now growing, posted a net loss of $39.7M in FY2025 (mostly a warrant mark-to-market), and tariffs cut its Q4 gross margin by about 600bp — [Owlet FY25 PR](https://s28.q4cdn.com/849126257/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/EX-99-1-Q4-25-Earnings-Press-Release-vF.pdf) [FILING].
- Anker's 2025 power-bank recalls (2.38M+ units) slowed profit growth below revenue growth — [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1181701403) [SECONDARY].
- Dyson's 2025 revenue fell 6.7%, which it blamed on US tariffs, currency moves and weak consumer confidence, while reports cite pressure from Chinese rivals — [ChannelNews AU](https://www.channelnews.com.au/dyson-family-takes-a1-43-billion-dividend-as-revenue-slides-and-chinese-rivals-attack/), [Dyson 2025 results](https://www.dyson.co.uk/discover/news/press-releases/dyson-financial-results-2025).
- Therabody's CEO Monty Sharma cited tariff costs and consumer cutbacks affecting 2025 holiday sales — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp-video/mmvo252563013607) [SECONDARY].

### Inferences
- **Why connected fitness failed while wearables won:** Peloton, Mirror and Tonal sold $1,500–$6,700 single-use, space-consuming devices whose demand was pulled forward by lockdowns. Oura and Whoop sell $200–$400 always-worn devices with daily-use data and low marginal cost per subscriber. Churn and post-pandemic demand normalization hit the high-ticket models hardest.
- Peloton shows that a subscription base can be turned profitable through price increases and cost cuts, but a shrinking install base caps revenue. Its FY2026 profit is a turnaround, not a return to growth.
- Mirror is the clearest case of acquirer value destruction: about 89% of the purchase price was written off within about 2.5 years. Retail Dive's figure is a "$443M impairment"; the Yahoo figure is $442.7M post-tax. The parent brand's community and retail strength did not transfer to hardware.
- Tariffs (2025) are a new cross-cutting headwind for hardware brands manufacturing in China (Owlet, Therabody, Dyson, Anker's US business).

### Gaps
- No exact date or official confirmation of the Tonal 35% layoff; the search budget ran out before I could verify it. There is no Tonal revenue figure.
- Whether Lululemon completed a sale of Mirror, or formally ended the hardware, could not be confirmed with a 2025–2026 source.
- I did not find a source for Peloton's Amazon/third-party retail channel share (it began selling via Amazon and Dick's around 2022, but this is unsourced here).
