# Most Successful Ecommerce-Native (DTC-Born) Brands: Beauty, Personal Care, Health & Wellness

Research date: 2026-11-16. Evidence labels used throughout:
- **[Audited/SEC]** = SEC filing or public-company earnings release (audited annual or reviewed quarterly)
- **[Acquirer-disclosed]** = figure stated in an acquirer's press release or filing
- **[Company-reported]** = private company's own statement (not audited publicly)
- **[Third-party report]** = reputable press citing unnamed sources (Reuters, BoF, Axios, Puck)
- **[Estimate]** = data-vendor or analyst model (Sacra, EightX, Particl, Grips, ecdb). Directional only.
- **[Unverified]** = low-quality or conflicting source; do not headline.

Note: the web search budget ran out before I could collect list prices for every brand. Prices below come from brand sites, acquirer releases, or press. Where none was found, the price is listed as a gap.

## Q1. Which DTC-born beauty / personal care / wellness brands have the strongest evidence of success?

### Takeaway
By hard, verifiable evidence, the clear #1 is **Hims & Hers**: a public telehealth subscription business with $2.35B FY2025 revenue (+59%), guided to about $3.1B in 2026, though margins collapsed in 2026. Next are **APR/Medicube** (KRX-listed, about US$1.05B 2025 revenue at a ~24% operating margin, the #1 beauty seller on TikTok Shop), **Mammoth Brands/Harry's** ($835M 2024 revenue, company-reported), and **rhode**: $212M LTM sales when e.l.f. agreed to buy it for up to $1B, then ~$390M in its first fiscal year under e.l.f. The best "exit with a price" evidence comes from acquirer disclosures: rhode (up to $1B), Touchland ($700M + $180M earn-out on $130M sales and $55M EBITDA), DECIEM ($2.2B valuation), Kylie ($1.2B valuation), Dollar Shave Club (~$1B), Drunk Elephant ($845M), Billie ($310M) and Native ($100M). Several of those exits later stumbled.

### Inferences
- These multiples are my own arithmetic from disclosed figures. Touchland: ~5.4x TTM sales and ~12.7x EBITDA at the $700M upfront (~6.8x sales including the full earn-out). rhode: ~3.8x (company-stated) at $800M upfront. Billie: ~3.4x. Drunk Elephant: ~8x on estimated ~$100M sales. Strategic buyers paid 3–6x sales for profitable, fast-growing DTC-born personal care brands in 2021–2025. The richest multiple went to the one with disclosed ~42% EBITDA margins (Touchland: $55M on $130M).
- Hims is the only DTC-born brand in this set with audited, multi-billion-dollar revenue. It shows that telehealth subscription (high ARPU: $83–105/month) can scale far beyond beauty-product DTC. The cost is heavy marketing (~39% of revenue in 2025) and regulatory or drug-supply risk (see Q4).
- Celebrity and creator brands (rhode, Rare, Fenty, Kylie, Huda, Skims) dominate the top of beauty by scale. rhode is the only one with an audited acquirer trail showing post-deal acceleration (+80%). Rare and Fenty both failed to clear sale processes at their target prices, which suggests buyers discount celebrity brands without verified profitability.
- APR/Medicube shows the new playbook: TikTok Shop plus Amazon plus devices, with a ~24% operating margin at ~US$1B revenue. Whether APR counts as "DTC-born" should be confirmed (see Gaps).

## Q2. What does each sell, at what price, and how do they acquire customers (social/creators, TikTok Shop, subscription)?

### Takeaway
Three models dominate. (1) **Subscription telehealth** (Hims & Hers, Ro, Eucalyptus): Rx and OTC treatments at ~$83–105 average monthly revenue per subscriber, acquired through very heavy paid marketing. (2) **Celebrity or creator-led color cosmetics and skincare** (rhode, Rare, Fenty, Kylie, Huda): $16–$32 hero SKUs, one-time purchase, social-first and then fast into Sephora/Ulta. (3) **Replenishable personal care and supplements** (Harry's, DSC, Billie, Native, Manscaped, AG1, Touchland): subscription or habitual repurchase, mostly migrating into mass retail and Costco once scaled. TikTok Shop has become a major beauty channel (near $1B/quarter in US beauty GMV), led by K-beauty (Medicube) rather than legacy DTC darlings.

## Q3. What are the typical gross margins and repeat-purchase dynamics in beauty/personal care/supplements vs other categories?

### Takeaway
Public beauty and personal care companies cluster at ~70–74% gross margin (e.l.f. 71%, Hims 74% in FY2025, Olaplex ~68–71%), against ~50–65% for apparel in vendor benchmarks. Beauty also has far lower return rates (4–15% vs ~24–26% for apparel). Supplements and consumables show the highest repeat-purchase rates (~29–45% 12-month repeat vs ~21–35% for beauty and a ~28% cross-vertical average). Operating margins are much thinner than gross margins because of marketing spend. All non-public-company benchmarks come from vendor blogs and are directional.

### Cited Findings
- **e.l.f. Beauty**: FY2026 gross margin 71% (-~50bp); adj. EBITDA margin 20%. [SEC 8-K] — [e.l.f. Q4 FY2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001600033/000160003326000018/q42026er-991.htm)

The 2026 shift to branded GLP-1s and international acquisitions coincides with the drop, though the release does not attribute it explicitly. - **Repeat purchase**: 12-month repeat rates are ~29–45% for supplements and consumables, ~21–35% for beauty, and ~9–18% for jewelry and electronics. ### Inferences
- Beauty's structural advantages over apparel (+10–20pts gross margin, a fraction of the returns, small ship-light SKUs) explain why so many DTC-born beauty brands reached $100M+ and attractive exits. The binding constraint is customer acquisition cost. Hims spent ~39% of revenue on marketing in 2025, and public beauty operating margins are thin.
- Supplements and Rx telehealth have the best repeat economics (daily-use, subscription-friendly). Color cosmetics rely more on new launches and virality, which fits the celebrity/creator model and TikTok Shop.
- The Hims margin collapse from 79% to 57% shows that telehealth margins depend on the drug mix. Compounded drugs carried high margins; branded GLP-1s and acquired international businesses carry lower ones.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) benchmark study on DTC gross margins or repeat rates by category was found. All category ranges come from agency or vendor blogs and conflict at the edges.
- No disclosed subscription retention or churn figures for Hims, Ro, AG1 or DSC.

## Q4. Which notable brands stumbled and why?

### Takeaway
The stumbles cluster around four causes: (1) **loss of product or brand heat after scaling into wholesale and stores** (Glossier, Drunk Elephant, Olaplex); (2) **celebrity brands that couldn't sustain post-deal growth** (Kylie under Coty, SKKN by Kim); (3) **subscription razors commoditizing** (Dollar Shave Club's Unilever exit); and (4) **telehealth regulatory and drug-supply risk** (Hims & Hers' 2026 margin collapse and losses after the compounded-GLP-1 crackdown). Several leaders (Rare Beauty, Fenty) also failed to clear sales at their target valuations.

### Inferences
- The highest exit prices (DSC ~$1B, Drunk Elephant $845M, Kylie $1.2B valuation) were paid at or near peak brand heat. - Glossier's reset (closing most stores, cutting SKUs, focusing on heroes) and Olaplex's stabilization show that brand heat in beauty decays quickly once a brand goes broad. Brands that keep hero-SKU focus and creator relevance (rhode, The Ordinary) kept growing.
- Telehealth's growth comes with regulatory and pharma-partner risk that pure product brands don't face. Hims' 2026 margin and EBITDA reset is the clearest example.
