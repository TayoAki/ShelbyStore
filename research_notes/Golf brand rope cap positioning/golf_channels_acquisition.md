Research date: 2026-11-16. US focus. Each figure carries a grade:
- **[Company-reported]**: the brand, league or creator said it (self-interested, not audited)
- **[Press-reported]**: independent reporting by a named outlet (Front Office Sports, CNN, GOLF.com, etc.)
- **[Third-party estimate]**: tracker or calculator output (Social Blade, Influencer Marketing Hub calculator, Feedspot, creatordb, OpenSponsorship, Fospha)

## 1. The golf creator landscape: audience sizes, demographics and merch businesses

### Takeaway
Golf YouTube is now a mass-reach channel. Rick Shiels, Bryson DeChambeau, Good Good, Grant Horvat and Bob Does Sports each have about 1.4M to 3.1M subscribers, and the biggest Instagram reach belongs to female creators (Paige Spiranac at about 4.1M). Hats are the hero product of creator merch: Good Good's hats led best-seller lists at Dick's and Golf Galaxy in 2025, and Bob Does Sports (Breezy Golf) and Barstool's Fore Play sell rope caps. A new rope-cap brand would be competing for shelf and feed space with creator-owned hat lines that already have their own distribution.

### Inferences
- The largest creators already sell hats through their own brands (Good Good, Breezy/Bob Does Sports, Barstool). A one-product rope-cap brand is unlikely to win a hat deal with them, because they are competitors. Better targets are creators with no hat line of their own: mid-tier YouTubers (Peter Finch, Micah Morris), female creators with large Instagram or TikTok reach (Tisha Alyn, Gabby, Lucy Robson), and micro-creators.
- Good Good's 2026 ad crisis shows how fast retail can disappear when a brand depends on one creator: four national chains dropped it within a week. Pro shops and the brand's own website held up. A small brand should spread its creator spend across many people and check each creator's brand safety before signing.
- Even in a crisis week, two-thirds of Good Good's website buyers were new customers. A creator's audience can bring a steady flow of first-time buyers to a brand's own store, which supports building on DTC and pro shops rather than big-box retail.

### Gaps
- I found no audited revenue for any creator merch business, and no hat-level sales for Good Good.
- I found no per-creator audience demographics (age, gender, location) from YouTube Analytics or any media kit.
- The 2025 and 2026 Creator Classic audience figures did not turn up. Only 2024 was found.
- Current TikTok follower counts for Micah Morris, Peter Finch and Paige Spiranac did not turn up in a reliable source.

## 2. Golf influencer economics: rates, affiliate and gifting programs, collabs and results

### Takeaway
Calculator estimates put a top golf creator's Instagram post at about $2.7K–$11K, and micro-creators at a few hundred dollars. Golf affiliate commissions mostly run 5–15%, and some ambassador tiers go up to 30%. Brands commonly run ambassador programs on platforms like Upfluence or GRIN, offering discount codes, commission and free gear. Bad Birdie's early growth reportedly came from sending free product to golf influencers who posted without being paid, but that account comes from secondary sources. No source offered audited return figures for headwear campaigns.

### Cited Findings
- Estimated value of one sponsored Instagram post (April 2026, Influencer Marketing Hub calculator applied to Social Blade follower counts): Paige Spiranac $10,837; Grace Charis $9,339; Lucy Robson $3,736; Rick Shiels $3,411; Grant Horvat $3,266; Bob Does Sports $2,944; Garrett Clark $2,669. For pros, Scottie Scheffler is $5,126 and Max Homa $3,062. The article calls these "estimates and intended for comparative analysis only." **[Third-party estimate]** — [RotoGrinders](https://rotogrinders.com/articles/golf-influencers-vs-pro-golfers-4198164)
- An older Vegas Insider analysis, about 2.5 years old, puts the average golf influencer at about $4,837 per Instagram post and the average pro at about $994. It estimates Spiranac at $8,477–$12,716 per post and Alex Romo, with just under 40K followers, at $207–$311. **[Third-party estimate]** — [Vegas Insider](https://www.vegasinsider.com/analysis-of-golf-influencers-and-professional-golf-players-social-earnings/)
- OpenSponsorship's golf roster averages about $410–$4,000 per deal. For one creator, Golf Girl Games, the platform estimates $7,200 per social post, $10,800 per appearance and $64,800 for an ambassador campaign. **[Third-party estimate]** — [OpenSponsorship golf](https://opensponsorship.com/categories/golf); [Golf Girl Games](https://opensponsorship.com/profiles/golf-girl-games)
- A 2026 rate guide gives these Instagram ranges: micro-creators (10K–100K followers) $300–$2,500 per post, and mid-tier (100K–500K) $2,000–$12,000. It also claims golf creators charge 30–50% more than general sports creators with the same audience size. **[Unverified rate guide]** — [InfluencerFee](https://influencerfee.com/blog/sports-influencer-rates/)
- Bob Does Sports charged $95 per Cameo video in 2023 and recorded 20–30 a day, with Cameo taking 25%. This gives a rough sense of what a top creator's personal shout-out costs. **[Press-reported]** — [GOLF.com](https://golf.com/lifestyle/bob-does-sports-business-unlikely-rise/)
- **Affiliate and ambassador terms:**
  - Live Forever Golf pays 8% per sale **[Network listing]** — [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/live-forever-golf-affiliate-program/)
  - TaylorMade reportedly pays 5–6% through Impact with a 30-day cookie **[Unverified listicle]** — [Tenngrand](https://tenngrand.com/15-best-golf-affiliate-programs-ranked-by-real-commission-math-payout-potential/)
  - Blue Tees' ambassador program reportedly pays 10% rising to 30%, plus store credit, early access and reposts **[Unverified, GRIN listing]** — [GRIN](https://app.grin.ai/brands/blueteesgolf.com)
  - William Murray Golf's "Collective" has a tier with a 15% customer discount code plus affiliate payouts, and a creator tier paying 12–15% commission with a 20% discount code and milestone gifts **[Company-reported]** — [William Murray Golf](https://williammurraygolf.com/pages/william-murray-collective)
- Bad Birdie's "Bad Ambassador" program runs on Upfluence. Ambassadors get a unique discount code, earnings are tracked automatically, and payouts take 2–6 weeks to allow for returns. The page lists no commission rate or follower minimum. A separate Members program gives 10% back. **[Company-reported]** — [Bad Birdie ambassadors](https://badbirdiegolf.com/pages/ambassadors)
- Bad Birdie's founder reportedly contacted golf influencers on social media and sent them free product, and they posted about it without payment. - One agency says a DTC golf brand doubled monthly revenue from $250K to $500K in 60 days by rebuilding its Meta account around one hero product and creator-led content. No ROAS was given. **[Unverified, agency marketing]** — [Your Marketing Partners](https://yourmarketingpartners.com/case-studies/golf-ecom-250k-to-500k)
- **Creator collaboration risk:** the Good Good x Callaway driver ad (Aug 2026) cost Good Good its Callaway partnership, four national retailers and two title sponsorships within about a week. **[Press-reported]** — [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/27/sport/good-good-callaway-advertisement)

### Inferences
- With one $38–52 product and roughly a $20–30 gross margin per unit (the margin is an assumption, not sourced), a $3K+ flat-fee post from a top creator needs about 100–150 incremental sales just to break even. Gifting plus 10–15% commission to many micro-creators (10K–100K followers, around $300–$2,500 per post if paid) fits a small budget better. That matches the free-product approach Bad Birdie reportedly used early on.
- Golf brands commonly offer a discount code of 15–20% plus a commission of 10–15%. A rope cap at $38–52 can carry that, but it leaves little room for a paid flat fee on top.
- The calculator estimates are for Instagram only. Integrated YouTube segments from the major golf channels probably cost much more, but I found no source to confirm that.

### Gaps
- I found no disclosed rate cards or real deal terms for YouTube integrations with the major golf channels.
- No brand published audited results (sales, CAC or ROAS) from a golf creator gifting or affiliate program for headwear.
- I could not confirm Bad Birdie's influencer-seeding story or its revenue path ("$72K to $7M") in a primary interview.

## 3. Culture moments: Masters, PGA/LIV/TGL, Ryder Cup, YouTube matches and viral hats

### Takeaway
Hats are the product most tied to golf's culture moments. Tournament novelty hats sell out fast: the Phoenix Open's upside-down "Sixteen" hat sold out on the first day in both 2025 and 2026, and the Masters' PIMENTO hat now resells for hundreds of dollars. The current trend in golf headwear (cheeky off-center text, pastel or neon colors, semi-flat bills, rope accents, upside-down wear) suits a rope-cap brand that can design around specific moments. YouTube matches such as the Creator Classic and Break 50 now draw audiences in the millions, many of them young.

### Cited Findings
- **The headwear trend (Front Office Sports, Aug 2026):** "B.U.D." (backward or upside-down) hats, pastel or neon colors, cheeky sayings set off-center on the front panel, semi-flat bills and rope accents. Designers and pros date the look to 2021–2022. Dallas brand True Brvnd claims it invented the B.U.D. design in 2020, and Ahead supplies hats to several PGA Tour events. Hats are described as cheaper and quicker to produce than other apparel. The Tour is adding more of these styles, and camo, PVC patches and flat embroidery are named as coming next. **[Press-reported; brand claims Company-reported]** — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/trendy-golf-hat-divide-is-more-than-a-fashion-fight/)
- **Phoenix Open:** "The 2025 WM Phoenix Open was the first time we carried upside-down hats ... we sold out by the first day of the tournament," and "We brought in additional inventory this year and sold out quickly again." (Ariana Sypert, PGA Tour) **[Company-reported, via press]** — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/trendy-golf-hat-divide-is-more-than-a-fashion-fight/)
- **Masters:** the Masters souvenir business is a "$70+ million" industry. The orange PIMENTO hat sold out and resells for hundreds of dollars. **[Press-reported]** — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/trendy-golf-hat-divide-is-more-than-a-fashion-fight/). At the 2025 Masters, amateur José Luis Ballester's upside-down Arizona State "Sun Devils" hat angered fans on social media and became a flashpoint in the hat debate **[Press-reported]** — [Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/golf/masters-amateur-sun-devils-hat-golf-fans-mad-augusta-national). A fan's custom "EGG SALAD" rope-style hat reportedly went viral at the 2026 Masters **[Unverified, retailer blog]** — [Fremont Clothing](https://fremontclothing.com/the-egg-salad-hat-that-broke-the-internet-at-the-2026-masters/)
- **Ryder Cup 2025 at Bethpage:** accessories supplier PRG Golf reported the largest single-event order in its 20-year history and more than 50,000 accessories sold on-site, with record sell-through **[Company-reported]** — [Golf Business News](https://golfbusinessnews.com/news/retail-new-products-news/prg-golf-celebrates-landmark-ryder-cup-as-official-team-supplier). The official shop included headwear from New Era, Nike, Ralph Lauren, Peter Millar, Callaway and others, and the cap lineup included rope caps **[Press-reported]** — [GOLF.com](https://golf.com/gear/golf-accessories/ryder-cup-hats-selling-fast/); [Fairway Jockey listing](https://fairwayjockey.com/collections/headwear/products/2025-ryder-cup-navy-aerosphere-tech-fabric-rope-cap). - **YouTube matches:** the 2024 Creator Classic drew more than 2M total views and 118K peak concurrent viewers, was the No. 2 trending show on YouTube in its first 24 hours, and was the Tour's most-viewed YouTube video of 2024. It also gave the Tour's channel its biggest single day of subscriber growth. **[Company-reported, PGA Tour, via press]** — [GOLF.com](https://golf.com/news/pga-tour-viewership-data-creator-classic/); [MyGolfSpy](https://mygolfspy.com/youtube/creator-classic-aces-its-debut/). For Break 50 view counts, see Section 1.
- **Riding moments by sponsorship (and the risk):** Good Good was title sponsor of a PGA Tour event, the Good Good Championship in Austin (Nov 12–15, 2026, $6M purse), and was set to sponsor *Big Break x Good Good* on Golf Channel. It gave up both after the August 2026 ad controversy **[Press-reported]** — [Front Office Sports / Yahoo](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/good-good-golf-business-usual-171849545.html)
- **Malbon:** a vest reading simply "Malbon" set off a debate about whether it belonged on the course. The brand says the story drew more than 4B impressions online. - **Rope hats specifically:** golf rope hats returned through golf lifestyle brands and vintage culture, and Callaway is credited with bringing the style back on tour. ### Inferences
- Phoenix Open "Sixteen," PIMENTO and Egg Salad show what sells around a moment: an inside joke about a specific tournament, in limited quantity, on a novelty hat. A rope-cap brand can plan limited drops around golf's calendar (Phoenix Open in February, Masters week in April, US Open and Open Championship in summer, Ryder Cup in odd years, the Creator Classic). Event names and logos are trademarked, so designs should use unlicensed references and inside jokes instead (trademark risk is an inference, not sourced).
- Official licensing (Ryder Cup, PGA Tour tents) is controlled by established suppliers such as PRG, New Era and Ahead. A small brand will reach these moments more realistically through social content and timed launches than through official merch tents.
- The upside-down and cheeky-text look is a fashion debate in itself. Picking a side (traditionalist rope cap or irreverent B.U.D.-friendly cap) can be part of the brand's positioning.

### Gaps
- I found no sales or brand-lift data on LIV or TGL headwear, and no TGL audience demographics.
- I found no documented case of a player-worn hat selling out a small brand after a major, though such cases may exist.
- 2025 and 2026 Creator Classic viewership was not found.

## 4. Paid and owned channels: Meta/TikTok ads, TikTok Shop, email/SMS, newsletters, podcasts, NIL, clubs and venues

### Takeaway
The only golf-specific paid-social data is vendor and agency case studies. They suggest TikTok and Meta both work for golf, that last-click attribution badly undercounts paid social, and that most TikTok-driven buyers are new customers. Benchmarks put TikTok apparel ROAS at a median of about 2.7x. I found no public golf category data for TikTok Shop, newsletter rate cards (Fried Egg, No Laying Up) or podcast ad rates. Golf NIL deals mostly take the form of ambassador rosters and deals with juniors, and pro shops and golf courses are a proven, resilient sales channel.

### Cited Findings
- **Callaway (Fospha case study, US, H2 2025 vs H2 2024):** TikTok spend up 18x with TikTok ROAS up 146%; Meta ROAS up 47% on 12% more spend; blended revenue up 15% and blended ROAS up 6%. Last-click attribution captured only about 1/24 of the paid-social revenue Fospha's model credited (18x undercounting for Meta, 95x for TikTok), and about 85% of TikTok conversions were new customers. Fospha's model is proprietary and unvalidated, and Callaway sells mainly equipment. **[Third-party estimate, vendor case study]** — [Fospha](https://www.fospha.com/case-studies/callaway-golf)
- **ROAS benchmarks:** Triple Whale puts the median TikTok ROAS for apparel and accessories at 2.70 **[Third-party estimate]** — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/tiktok-benchmarks). Another benchmark puts median ROAS at 1.86 on Meta and 1.41 on TikTok, with apparel often at 2.8–3.5x on TikTok **[Unverified]** — [Rule1](https://rule1.ai/articles/roas-benchmarks)
- An agency reports a golf equipment brand's Meta ROAS rising from 2.02 to 2.95 as spend increased **[Unverified, agency marketing]** — [1Digital Agency](https://www.1digitalagency.com/cutting-edge-golf-equipment-meets-cutting-edge-social-media-marketing/)

### Inferences
- For a one-product brand, Meta and TikTok creative built around one hero product is the paid-social approach with the most (agency-level) evidence. Because last-click attribution undercounts paid social heavily, a small brand should judge results on blended new-customer CAC or MER, plus a "how did you hear about us" question after purchase, not platform ROAS alone.
- Pro shops have both resilience (Good Good) and early-traction evidence (Seamus, Sunday Golf). A local wholesale push to 10–30 courses and clubs, where staff and members wear the hat, is a sensible complement to DTC. Club logo co-branding is an inference, not sourced.
- College NIL in golf is mostly ambassador rosters and small deals. Seeding hats to a few college or junior golfers with strong social followings is cheap, but the Division I disclosure rule ($600+) should be kept in mind.

## 5. How the successful golf DTC brands acquired their first customers and scaled

### Takeaway
The founder stories share a pattern. Each brand started with a distinctive product or point of view (a fun, loud aesthetic; streetwear golf; Black golf culture; a unique par-3 bag; tartan headcovers). Early customers came through the founder's own community and social accounts, free product for influencers, or resort and club pro shops. Brands scaled through a big moment (Shark Tank, a Nike or Jordan collaboration, celebrity friends) and then wholesale. Good Good is the exception: it built the audience first, on YouTube, and hats became its leading product.

### Cited Findings
- **Malbon:** it began as an Instagram account showing "golf's cooler side," shaped by Stephen Malbon's background in streetwear publishing (Frank151). Nike Golf noticed the account and hired him to consult, and the first shop opened on Fairfax Avenue in 2017. The early community was a crew of LA friends (Eric Koston, ScHoolboy Q, George Lopez) **[Press-reported founder interviews]** — [MBS Group](https://www.thembsgroup.co.uk/internal/the-green-is-the-common-ground-in-conversation-with-stephen-malbon-founder-of-malbon-golf/); [Hypebeast 2019](https://hypebeast.com/2019/4/stephen-malbon-golf-interview-nike). By 2025, revenue was "approaching $200 million," it had raised $33M led by Anthos Capital, products cost about $68–$200, and wholesale included Nordstrom. It hired Nike alumni as CEO, CMO and CPO and expanded into "And golf" lines (Hunt Club, Tackle, run clubs, a Grateful Dead capsule) **[Revenue Company-reported via Glossy]** — [Glossy 50, 2025](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/stephen-and-erica-malbon-malbon-glossy-50-2025/)
- **Bad Birdie:** founded by Jason Richardson as a side project to make golf apparel "fun." He appeared on Shark Tank in 2020, where Robert Herjavec offered $300K for 25%, and COVID-era golf demand helped growth **[Press-reported]** — [AZ Big Media, 2022](https://azbigmedia.com/business/how-shark-tank-helped-drive-scottsdales-bad-birdie/). Early tactics reportedly included sending free product to golf influencers and building a meme- and customer-photo-led Instagram. Revenue reportedly grew from $72K to about $7M in four years **[Unverified]** — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/shark-tank-helped-bad-birdie-turn-side-hustle-7m-yearly-corey-turner)
- **Eastside Golf:** founded June 1, 2019. Olajuwon Ajanaku spent his savings and 401(k) and sold his crypto to make the first sweatshirts, tees and socks. Through Jordan Brand's Gentry Humphrey, the founders pitched Michael Jordan directly, which led to a Jordan Brand partnership with nine collaborations so far **[Press-reported founder interview, Aug 2026]** — [TheGrio](https://thegrio.com/2026/08/07/eastside-golf-michael-jordan-pitch/). It later opened its first US storefront in Detroit **[Press-reported]** — [The Golf Wire](https://thegolfwire.com/eastside-golf-detroit)
- **Metalwood Studio:** founded by Cole Young, a former Loyola Marymount golfer, around a 1990s and early-2000s golf aesthetic. Founding-year sources conflict (2017 vs 2020). Its La Brea store works as a hangout, and early fans included pro skaters such as Koston and Malto. An adidas MC70 shoe collaboration followed in 2026 **[Press-reported]** — [The Grassy Issue](https://thegrassyissue.com/drops/brand-to-know-metalwood-studio); [Garrett Leight interview](https://garrettleight.eu/blogs/stories/meet-the-man-making-outdated-clubs-cool); [Hypebeast LA golf](https://hypebeast.com/2023/3/los-angeles-golf-culture-fashion-travel-city-guide)
- **Sunday Golf:** launched Aug 2020. "The par 3 golf bag helped us get our initial customers because it's truly a unique product" (co-founder Ronan Galvin). It ran paid Facebook and TikTok campaigns, signed a junior NIL deal and crowdfunded on Wefunder. By 2023 it had sold 100K+ bags, earned $5M revenue in 2022 (40% YoY growth) and was in 1,200+ doors **[Company-reported via press]** — [Business of San Diego, Aug 2023](https://businessofsandiego.substack.com/p/sunday-golf-san-diegos-15-million). It reported $6.5M+ net revenue for 2023 **[Company-reported, Wefunder]** — [Wefunder](https://wefunder.com/sundaygolf)
- **Seamus Golf:** started in 2011 in a garage by Akbar and Megan Chisti. Akbar had worked summers in the Bandon Dunes pro shop, and Bandon became one of the earliest accounts. Highlands Golf Club placed an early 24-headcover order. Tour pros (Matt Kuchar, Ryan Moore) using the headcovers gave it visibility **[Press-reported]** — [LINKS Magazine](https://linksmagazine.com/?p=52812); [Seamus Our Story](https://seamusgolf.com/pages/our-story)
- **Good Good:** launched as a YouTube channel in July 2020, with merch from 2021. It grew from about 800 to 4,000 retail doors in a year and led hat best-seller lists in 2025 (see Section 1) **[Company-reported]** — [Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/trendy-golf-hat-divide-is-more-than-a-fashion-fight/)

### Inferences
- None of these brands grew mainly through paid ads at the start. Their first customers came from the founders' own networks and communities (Malbon's LA crew, Metalwood's skaters, Seamus's Bandon Dunes, Eastside's culture-first story), from free product for influencers (Bad Birdie, reportedly) or from a product that stood out on its own (Sunday Golf). A rope-cap brand needs a sharp point of view, not just "a nice rope hat," to repeat this.
- The breakout moments all came from outside paid media: a Nike or Jordan connection, Shark Tank, tour pros using the product, Bill Murray at the Ryder Cup. A small brand can create its own version by seeding tour caddies, college players and mid-tier creators. Whether this pays off can't be predicted, so it should run alongside steady DTC and pro-shop sales.
- At $68–$200, Malbon sits well above a $38–52 rope cap, while novelty hats on TikTok Shop sell at around $32. The rope cap's price places it between the two, so the brand needs a clear quality or design story to justify it against cheaper novelty hats.

### Gaps
- Founder interviews with first-customer detail (first 100 orders, early CAC) were not found for Malbon, Eastside or Metalwood. Podcasts exist (Chasin' Birdies with Stephen Malbon, Butternomics with Olajuwon Ajanaku, The Hole Story with Seamus), but I did not transcribe them.
- I found no primary source on Bad Birdie's influencer seeding or its revenue path.
- No Modern Retail coverage of golf DTC customer acquisition turned up in searches.
