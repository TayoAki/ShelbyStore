# Golf Apparel and Headwear Brand Landscape by Segment, and the Best Fit for a $38–52 Single-Product Rope Cap Brand

Research date: 2026-11-16. Evidence grades used throughout:
- **A: audited/filed.** SEC filings, public-company earnings releases, annual reports.
- **B: company-reported.** Press releases, founder or executive statements, the brand's own website.
- **C: third-party estimate.** PitchBook, Tracxn, ZoomInfo, LeadIQ, Growjo, market-research firms, trade-press estimates.
- **D: unverified.** A single secondary source, with figures that conflict or have no primary source.

**How cap prices were collected.** Cap prices come from each brand's public Shopify product feed (`/products.json`), pulled on 2026-11-16. The figure is the current selling price; where a "compare-at" price was present, it is noted as the list price. This snapshot falls in the pre-Black-Friday promotional window, so some prices are markdowns. Brands not on Shopify, or that blocked the request (G/FORE, Peter Millar, Titleist, Rhoback, Manors, Radda, Random Golf Club, Imperial, Pukka, Ahead), have no scraped prices and are listed under Gaps.

## 1. For each brand: what it sells, cap price points, success evidence (graded), and growth story

### Takeaway
Ranked by **verifiable** evidence, the strongest players are the corporately owned incumbents, and their figures are filed:
- **Titleist/FootJoy** (Acushnet): $2.56B in FY2025 sales; its gear segment, which includes headwear, is about $245M.
- **TravisMathew**: inside Callaway's $685.0M Apparel, Gear and Other segment.
- **Peter Millar/G/FORE**: inside Richemont's €2.8B "Other" division.

Next come the private brands whose figures are company-reported:
- **Rhoback**: more than $150M revenue in 2025, profitable, per its own press release.
- **Malbon**: a $33M raise led by Anthos; revenue "approaches $200M," which is the press's framing, not an audited figure.
- **Johnnie-O**: a $108M minority investment in 2022.
- **Good Good**: about 800 to 4,000 retail doors in one year.

Most streetwear-golf and hat-led brands have only third-party estimates or no financial evidence at all.

In headwear specifically, the heritage makers own the green-grass custom-logo channel:
- **Imperial** (owned by CPC; bought Pukka in 2024).
- **Ahead** (owned by New Wave Group).

### Inferences
- **Rope caps are now commoditized at $35–46.** Good Good (121 rope SKUs, $35–40), Pins & Aces (79, list $35–40), Sunday Golf ($35–40), Barstool ($38), Linksoul ($38), Holderness & Bourne ($45) and Branded Bills (518 SKUs, $46) all sell them, and every major OEM rope hat is listed at TGW and Dick's. A new single-product rope-cap brand enters a crowded format, not an open niche.
- **The scraped prices form a clear ladder:**
  - Mass/merch: $35–40 (Good Good, Barstool, Pins & Aces, Sunday, Linksoul).
  - Premium lifestyle / classic: $42–48 (Bad Birdie, Holderness & Bourne, Branded Bills, Metalwood, Malbon's entry rope hat at $48).
  - Streetwear-golf "hype": $50–60 (Students $52, Malbon median $58, Eastside rope $50–60).
  - Premium technical: $79–89 (Melin Hydro rope).
  
  The $38–52 range spans the top of mass through the bottom of streetwear-golf.
- **Media or community-led brands show the clearest headwear wins.** Good Good's growth in doors and top-10 hats, and Barstool's merch tent, both point that way. Hat demand appears to follow audience and identity more than product spec.
- **Incumbents' apparel was flat to down in 2025** (FootJoy −1%, Callaway Apparel/Gear −1.4%). Acushnet gear (which includes headwear) grew 5.5–6%, and TravisMathew outgrew its segment. That suggests accessories, headwear and lifestyle-led lines outperformed core performance apparel in 2025.

## 2. Which segments are growing fastest, and which have the best economics for headwear? (incl. market size)

### Takeaway
Market-size estimates for global golf apparel in 2025 vary nearly 3x, from $3.5B to $9.5B, with projected growth of about 5.5–7% CAGR. All are third-party estimates, so treat them as directional.

The filed evidence shows legacy performance apparel flat to down in 2025 (FootJoy, Callaway apparel). Growth sits in:
- lifestyle-led and performance-lifestyle brands: TravisMathew; Rhoback at $150M+ and profitable;
- streetwear-golf: Malbon's reported scale and new capital;
- creator/media-led hat brands: Good Good's 5x door growth.

Headwear is golf apparel's #2 category by sales (Golf Datatech), but its unit sell-through softened in mid-2024. Its economics favor small brands: low unit cost, a low price point, and impulse purchase. But it is "a copycat league" with heavy markdowns.

  - performance-lifestyle (Rhoback, TravisMathew);
  - creator/media-led hat brands (Good Good, Barstool).
  
  Legacy performance apparel (FootJoy) and classic country-club apparel at the division level (Richemont "Other" flat in H1 FY26) look slower. This is an inference from mixed-grade evidence; no source measures segment growth directly.
- **Best economics for headwear.** At a $45 DTC price and an illustrative $8–14 landed cost for a premium rope cap with custom trims and low minimums (an inference that combines the vendor cost ranges above with freight), product-level gross margin would be roughly 70–80%. That is well above the ~47% public-DTC median. The binding constraint is CAC and AOV, not COGS.
- A single $38–52 hat produces low AOV, so contribution margin after shipping, payment fees and paid acquisition will be thin. Multi-hat bundles, colorways and drops raise AOV, and organic or community demand (the Good Good and Barstool model) reduces CAC.
- **The green-grass custom channel is not a good fit for a new DTC rope-cap brand.** Imperial and Ahead dominate through pro-shop custom-logo programs and acquisitions, and a single-SKU brand's own logo competes against club logos there.

## 3. Who buys golf caps? (age, gender, on-course vs lifestyle wearers, non-golfers; NGF participation and off-course data)

### Takeaway
US golf hit records in 2025:
- **48.1M** total participants (age 6+): 29.1M on-course, the 8th straight year of growth, plus 19M off-course only.
- **37.9M** off-course participants in total.
- **21.2M** non-golfers "very interested" in playing.

The growth audience is younger, more female and more diverse:
- Women are 28% of on-course golfers.
- Off-course-only participants are 43% female and 45% people of color (2024 data).
- 18–34-year-olds are the largest on-course age cohort.

No source measures cap buyers directly. The evidence suggests caps are bought heavily as lifestyle and identity items by off-course and non-golfer wearers, as well as by on-course golfers. Trade press says newer golfers "skew much younger and more female."

### Cited Findings

  - 48.1M Americans age 6+ played golf on or off course in 2025.
  - 29.1M played on a course.
  - 19M participated exclusively off-course (ranges, simulators, entertainment venues like Topgolf).
  - Source: [NGF Golf Industry Facts](https://www.ngf.org/the-clubhouse/golf-industry-research/); [SGB Media](https://sgbonline.com/exec-golf-participation-achieves-another-record-high-in-2025/)

## 4. Which segment and price position best fits a $38–52 single-product rope cap brand, and why?

The best fit is a **hat-led brand (segment d) built with modern/streetwear golf (segment a) design and community codes**, positioned as a **premium-lifestyle rope cap at about $44–48 core price**, with $52 reserved for limited or collab drops. It should not chase performance/tech, where Melin owns $79–89 and OEMs sell $30–50 with tour validation. It should not chase classic country club either: Imperial and Ahead own the pro-shop channel, and Holderness & Bourne and Linksoul already sell rope caps at $38–45.

The $38 floor collides with the mass and merch tier: Good Good, Barstool, Linksoul, Pins & Aces and Sunday Golf, all at $35–40 and heavily discounted. At $44–48 the brand sits with Bad Birdie, Holderness & Bourne, Branded Bills and Malbon's entry rope cap ($48). ### Inferences
**Why segment (d) with segment (a) styling wins for this brand:**
1. A single product means the brand is the hat. Hat-led brands (Melin, Imperial, Branded Bills, Good Good's headwear) prove a cap can carry a brand. Streetwear-golf brands (Malbon, Students, Metalwood) prove the aesthetic commands $48–60.
2. The growth audience (younger, female, off-course, non-golfers) buys on look and identity, not technical spec.
3. A one-SKU brand cannot credibly out-tech Melin or the OEMs, or out-heritage Imperial and Peter Millar.

**Why about $44–48 core:**
- At $38 the cap is priced against Barstool, Good Good and Linksoul, which have large audiences or wholesale scale and run frequent markdowns ($20–32 sale prices seen).
- At $52, buyers compare it with Students and Malbon, whose brand equity supports that price.
- $44–48 matches Holderness & Bourne ($45), Branded Bills ($46) and Malbon's entry rope ($48), signaling "premium but attainable."
- Use $52 for limited colorways or collabs; keep $38 as a bundle-effective price (e.g. 2 for $88–90), not the list price.

**Differentiation must come from somewhere other than the rope.** The rope detail alone is not defensible given 121 Good Good, 79 Pins & Aces and 518 Branded Bills rope SKUs. Candidate sources:
- a distinctive crown shape or fabric (e.g., washed cotton or corduroy rope, unstructured vintage fit);
- a recognizable icon;
- limited drops;
- a community or content angle (golf trips, munis, off-course or simulator culture).

**Channel.** Start DTC and social-led. Treat green-grass pro shops as a later, selective wholesale channel; Imperial and Ahead dominate custom club-logo programs there. Boutique golf-lifestyle retailers and off-course venues are likelier early wholesale partners. This is an inference; no source measured channel economics for a single-SKU brand.

**Economics check.** At $44–48 and an illustrative $8–14 landed unit cost, gross margin is about 70–80%. But single-hat AOV with $5–8 shipping and paid CAC is tight. Multi-color bundles, gifting moments and organic content are the levers. This is inferred from the cost and margin ranges cited above.
