# Repeat purchases, not hype, crown ecommerce winners

The largest ecommerce businesses are marketplaces that sell everything. Amazon leads with an estimated **~$830B in 2025 GMV** [Est.], followed by China's Alibaba, PDD and JD, Shopify's merchants combined (**$378.4B GMV** [Filed]) and Walmart (**~$150B** of ecommerce sales [Filed, via secondary]); their growth now comes from everyday consumables and video-led discovery, with TikTok Shop at an estimated **$64.3B** GMV [Est.]. Among brands born online, audited filings support a short list: Anker (**~US$4.2B**, chargers sold mostly on Amazon [Filed, via secondary]), Hims & Hers (**$2.35B** of telehealth subscriptions [Filed]), Revolve (**$1.23B** of women's fashion [Filed]), Ugreen (**~US$1.3B** of cables and chargers [Filed]), Oura (**$907.9M** of smart rings plus membership [Filed, via secondary]) and Warby Parker (**$871.9M** of eyewear [Filed]). Behind them sit company-reported billion-dollar revenue claims from Quince and Skims [Co.], a **$10.1B** Whoop valuation [Co.], and buyer-disclosed sales of Poppi ($1.95B), Alani Nu ($1.8B), rhode (up to $1B) and Touchland (up to $880M) [Filed]. The categories that produce lasting winners pair gross margins of 50% or more with low returns and a built-in reason to buy again (health subscriptions, beauty, pet food, beverages, membership wearables), while expensive, rarely bought products (mattresses, sofas, connected fitness) and single-novelty brands (Allbirds, Solo Stove, Prime) produced the largest collapses. Nearly every brand that scaled left pure DTC for stores, wholesale or Amazon, and strategic buyers paid roughly 3–6x sales only for brands that were profitable and growing. Apparel, Shelby's category, is the largest online category by dollars and yields the most DTC winners by count, but its ~23–25% online return rate [Est.] and middling repeat rates make it one of the hardest to profit in. The evidence says Shelby should run its core tee like a consumable (consistent fit, always in stock, built for reorders), use drops as small-batch tests and reasons to come back, and lean on creators and TikTok Shop to get around Meta ad prices that rose 9–10% a year in 2025 [Filed].

> **How to read the evidence grades.** Every figure carries one of four grades. **[Filed]** covers audited accounts, SEC, stock-exchange and Companies House filings, public-company earnings releases, IPO prospectuses, government statistics, and deal prices disclosed by a public acquirer; "[Filed, via secondary]" means the figure comes from a filing but was read in a news or analyst write-up rather than in the filing itself, and "[Filed-derived]" marks an independent dataset computed from public filings (Damodaran). **[Co.]** is a figure the company stated but nobody audited: private-company revenue claims, funding announcements, a target's financials as quoted by its acquirer, and a listed company's preliminary results. **[Est.]** is a third-party estimate from a tracker (Marketplace Pulse, Momentum Works, Charm.io, Sacra, PitchBook, ECDB, Grips), a vendor benchmark, a commissioned survey, or a press report citing unnamed sources. **[Unverified]** marks a figure seen only in a search snippet, a weak source or conflicting reports; it is shown for completeness and should not be relied on. "Calc" marks my own arithmetic on cited figures, and "[Observed]" marks a price sampled from a brand's public product feed on 2026-11-16. Research date: 2026-11-16.

In the US, Marketplace Pulse estimates Amazon's GMV at about $440B, roughly **36% of all US ecommerce** [Est.]. Secondary estimates range from 35.7% to 40.5% for Amazon and from 6.4% to 9.2% for Walmart, depending on method ([EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/amazon-and-shopify-now-control-nearly-half-of-u-s-e-commerce/); [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/274255/us-e-commerce-market-share/)) [Est.]. Ecommerce was **17.1% of US retail** in Q2 2026, up from 16.3% a year earlier ([Census](https://www.census.gov/retail/eCommerce.html); [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=Bebu)) [Filed]. For brand-owned commerce the most relevant benchmark is Shopify: its merchants' combined GMV grew 29%, while Amazon's GMV grew in the high single digits ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazon-gmv-surpassed-800-billion-in-2025)) [Est.].

What the giants sell varies by model, but their growth points the same way. By dollars, fashion (**~27%**) and electronics (**~24%**) are the largest online categories worldwide ([ECDB](https://ecdb.com/blog/leading-ecommerce-categories-in-top-markets/5134)) [Est., single vendor], yet the growth sits in consumables. Amazon's "everyday essentials" are about one in three US units and grew nearly twice as fast as other categories in 2025 ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazon-gmv-surpassed-800-billion-in-2025)) [Est.]. Digital Commerce 360 estimates 2025 US ecommerce at $1.234T, with mass merchants growing fastest (+14.4%), then food and beverage (+13%) and health and beauty (+11%) ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/article/us-ecommerce-sales/)) [Est.]. China's official statistics show online food sales up **14.5%** against **1.9%** for clothing in 2025 ([National Bureau of Statistics](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260120_1962354.html)) [Filed]. On TikTok Shop US, beauty and personal care leads at **19–22.5%** of tracked GMV depending on the tracker, ahead of womenswear (12.6%), menswear (8.1%), food (6.9%) and home supplies (4.1%) ([SmartScout](https://www.smartscout.com/blog/tiktok-shop-statistics-2026); [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/tiktok-shop-says-sales-from-u-s-small-businesses-climbed-66-in-2025/)) [Est.]. eBay has moved out of the price war into enthusiast goods, with pre-owned and refurbished items now over 40% of GMV ([eBay Q4 2025 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/02/18/ebay-ebay-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript/)) [Co.]. Shein's apparel share of revenue fell from 68.8% to 63.8% between 2023 and 2025 as other goods grew ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/shein-business-model-into-fashions-top-15-at-a-70-discount/2026082990051)) [Filed, via secondary].

The platforms are also converging on third-party marketplaces plus advertising. Amazon's third-party sellers rose from 60% to 69% of its GMV between 2019 and 2025 [Est.], and Shein's marketplace revenue went from 2.7% to 14.3% of the total [Filed, via secondary]. The cross-border low-price model broke when the US ended its duty-free de minimis exemption for low-value parcels from all countries, effective August 29, 2025 ([Federal Register](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2025-16802.pdf)) [Filed]. Temu's US daily active users fell about 48% between March and May 2025 ([Tech Buzz China](https://techbuzzchina.substack.com/p/temu-watch-9-temu-rebounds)) [Est.]. Shein's net profit fell from $3.37B to $2.06B ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/shein-hong-kong-ipo-financials)) [Filed, via secondary], and it listed at about **$26.5B** against a 2022 peak near $100B ([Khaleej Times/Reuters](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/sheins-hong-kong-ipo-pricing-values-company-at-265-billion-sources-say)) [Est.]. TikTok Shop, whose US GMV mostly ships from US sellers, barely noticed. The platform data shows online money moving toward cheap, frequently rebought goods found through video, and the brand winners below grew in the same place.

## Each category's winners built one hero product, then followed it into stores

### Fashion: cheap constant newness for Gen Z, or one hero product at mid-premium

Fashion's verified winners take one of two shapes. The first sells cheap, constant newness to Gen Z women. Princess Polly is "about half" of a.k.a. Brands' **$600M** FY2025 net sales ([a.k.a. Q4 2025 call](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/03/05/aka-brands-aka-q4-2025-earnings-transcript)) [Filed for the group; Co. for the share], with sampled prices of **$16–$135 and a $59 median** [Observed], and it markets "social-first" across more than 20 platforms including TikTok Live. Fashion Nova is large but unverifiable, with tracker estimates of $514M–$1.02B [Est.], and has paid $13.5M in FTC settlements, including $4.2M for hiding reviews below four stars ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/25/ftc-fines-online-shop-4m-for-hiding-reviews-below-4-stars/)). Revolve sits a rung higher at a **$299 average order value**. Among the public DTC-born fashion names researched, it is one of only two that were both growing and GAAP-profitable in 2025 [Filed].

The second shape sells one hero product at mid-premium prices. Warby Parker sells frames "starting at $95 including prescription lenses" and earned $324 of revenue per customer ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1504776/000150477626000006/wrby-20251231.htm)) [Filed]. Rothy's knit flats sell for **$39–$375 with a $150 median** [Observed], on 2025 revenue of $227.7M ([World Footwear](https://www.worldfootwear.com/news/alpargatas-posts-strong-full-year-results/11329.html)) [Co., via parent's results]. Brilliant Earth's engagement rings grew in 2025 on **13% more orders at an 8.2% lower order value**, as buyers shifted toward cheaper and lab-grown stones [Filed]. Quince sits between the two shapes. It sells "premium goods at affordable prices" direct from factories, forecasts weekly at product and size level, and orders small test batches before scaling winners ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/quince-secures-500-million-us-dollars-in-series-e-funding/2026031286830)) [Co.]; its newness is a way to collect data, not to create scarcity. Mejuri shows jewelry's repeat potential: **50% of revenue from repeat customers** across ~2M annual customers, yet "not yet profitable" in September 2023 after raising more than $100M ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/mejuri-announces-100-million-in-total-funding-new-stores-app-and-loyalty-program)) [Co.]. The apparel winners in [the earlier list](../docs/research/successful-stores.md), including Skims, Gymshark, True Classic, Vuori and Bombas, mostly sit on the hero-product side.

Allbirds is the category's defining failure. Revenue peaked at **$297.8M** in 2022 ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1653909/000165390923000030/bird-20221231.htm)) [Filed], then fell to **$152.5M** in 2025 with a $77.3M net loss and a going-concern warning ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1653909/000162828026022192/bird-20251231.htm)) [Filed]. In March 2026 it agreed to sell its assets for about **$39M**, after raising $348M in its 2021 IPO and briefly trading above a $4B valuation; its move into leggings, jackets and performance running "didn't connect with its core customers" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/30/allbirds-is-selling-for-39-million-it-raised-nearly-10-times-that-amount-in-its-ipo/)). Parade, a Gen Z underwear brand, sold on undisclosed terms in 2023, a year after a $200M valuation ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/08/15/parade-to-be-bought-by-fruit-of-the-loom-licensee-ariela.html)). Blue Nile went to Signet for **$360M** in cash, below its ~$500M take-private in 2017 and the ~$873M implied by an abandoned SPAC deal ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/signet-acquires-blue-nile-360-million/629173)) [Filed for the price]. The survivors added stores: Warby Parker has 323, Rothy's has 40 plus Nordstrom with wholesale "tripled" ([Retail Brew](https://www.retailbrew.com/stories/how-the-dtc-brand-rothys-grew-into-to-usd227-7-million-in-revenue-through-stores-and-wholesale)) [Co.], and Princess Polly has 13 US stores plus every Nordstrom US store [Co.]. Store-born brands moved the other way. Lululemon now does about **44%** of sales online (calc from analyst-reported figures; [Hudson Labs](https://www.hudson-labs.com/research/lululemon-stock-analysis-lulu)) [Filed, via secondary], and Aritzia did **32.1%** online in the first three quarters of FY2026 ([Aritzia](https://investors.aritzia.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Aritzia-Reports-Third-Quarter-Fiscal-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)) [Filed]. Mature fashion brands, whichever end they start from, settle at roughly a third to a half of sales online.

### Beauty and wellness: $10–$32 hero products and $83–$105 monthly prescriptions

Hims & Hers is the largest Western DTC-born brand with audited revenue: **$2.35B** in 2025 from 2.51M subscribers paying an average **$83 a month** for prescription and over-the-counter treatments, with $919.3M of marketing, or about **39% of revenue** ([Hims FY2025](https://investors.hims.com/news/news-details/2026/Hims--Hers-Health-Inc--Reports-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results/default.aspx)) [Filed]. The model cracked in 2026. Gross margin fell from 74% to **57%** and the company posted a $33.0M net loss in Q3 2026 ([Hims Q3 2026](https://investors.hims.com/news/news-details/2026/Hims--Hers-Health-Inc--Reports-Third-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx)) [Filed], after it shifted to branded weight-loss drugs once an FDA crackdown forced it to pull a **$49 compounded Wegovy** product ([Reuters via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2612099444)) [Est.].

The product brands win with cheap hero products that demonstrate well on video. rhode's Peptide Lip Tint costs **$20** and its Glazing Milk **$32** ([rhode](https://www.rhodeskin.com/)) [Observed]; Touchland's Power Mist costs **$10** ([Touchland](https://touchland.com/)) [Observed]. Native sold its deodorant at **$12** a stick when P&G bought it for about $100M in 2017, after Native had raised only about $500K ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/15/procter-gamble-just-bought-this-venture-backed-deodorant-startup-for-100-million-cash/?ncid=rss)) [Est., press]. APR's Medicube is the newest model: Korean skincare plus at-home devices sold through TikTok Shop and Amazon. APR reported about **US$1.05B** of 2025 revenue at an operating margin near **24%** (calc) [Co.], and Medicube was the **#1 US beauty brand on TikTok Shop** with $62.8M of sales in Q2 2026 ([Beauty Independent](https://www.beautyindependent.com/tiktok-shop-nears-1b-beauty-sales-second-straight-quarter/)) [Est., Charm.io].

Celebrity brands lead on scale but not on verified figures. Fenty is put at about **$450M** for 2024 ([Reuters via iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/2577005048)) [Est.] and Rare Beauty at **>$400M** in the 12 months to February 2024 ([BoF](https://businessoffashion.com/articles/beauty/selena-gomezs-rare-beauty-exploring-an-ipo-or-sale-as-revenue-crosses-400-million/)) [Est.]. Kylie Cosmetics was valued at about **$1.2B** when Coty bought 51% for $600M in 2019 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/11/18/coty-majority-owner-of-kylie-jenner-cosmetics/)) [Filed for the price, via press]. Strategic exits set the category's prices: DECIEM (The Ordinary) at a $2.2B valuation in 2021 ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/estee-lauder-snaps-up-controlling-stake-in-deciem-for-1b/595549)) [Filed for the price], Dollar Shave Club for about $1B in 2016 ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/unilever-sells-razor-subscription-brand-dollar-shave-club/697913)), Drunk Elephant for about $845M in 2019 ([BeautyMatter](https://beautymatter.com/articles/shiseido-acquires-drunk-elephant-for-845-million)), and Billie for $310M on about $90M of revenue in 2021 ([Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/schick-maker-edgewell-acquires-billie-for-310m/610772/)) [Filed for the price]. At scale these brands move into retail. Mammoth Brands (Harry's) reports about $835M of revenue [Co.] and now sells largely through retail ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/harrys-inc-rebrands-mammoth-brands/745123/)). AG1 projected **$600M** of revenue from a single greens powder ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/01/21/athletic-greens-launching-new-products-channels-2025/)) [Co., projection] and then entered Costco ([EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/ag1-estimate)) [Est.].

Beauty brands stumbled when their buzz faded after they went broad. Glossier's valuation was reportedly cut roughly in half from about $1.8B in 2021 ([Puck](https://puck.news/fashion/2025/4/glossiers-valuation-at-2-billion-four-years-ago-cut-in-half)) [Est.]; in 2026 it cut about a third of its staff and is closing 9 of 12 stores ([Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/glossier-to-shutter-nine-out-of-12-stores)). Shiseido took a **¥46.8bn** goodwill write-down on its Americas business, with Drunk Elephant's weak performance the main cause ([Cosmetics Business](https://www.cosmeticsbusiness.com/shiseido-share-price-soars-earnings-beat-2025-outlook-2026)) [Filed, via press]. Unilever sold its Dollar Shave Club majority in 2023 at an undisclosed price, Coty booked a loss of about **$71.1M** selling its SKKN by Kim stake ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/coty-sells-stake-kim-kardashians-230258305.html)), and Rare Beauty's sale process stalled when bankers could not get about $2B ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/09/10/selena-gomez-rare-beauty-sale-process)) [Est.]. Touchland drew the richest multiple, and Church & Dwight cited its "high level of brand loyalty and repeat purchase in line with category leaders" [Co.]. Buyers paid for verified profit and repeat buying, not peak buzz.

### Home: profits went to bedding and cookware, losses to bulky goods

Home produced few clean DTC winners. The largest audited one, Lovesac, made $697.1M in FY2026 but is really a showroom business: showrooms brought in $468.0M against $192.3M online, and net income was only $4.1M ([Lovesac 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1701758/000162828026021185/q4fy26pressrelease.htm)) [Filed]. Products that ship as ordinary parcels did better. Brooklinen has about $200M of revenue and is profitable ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/?p=161508)) [Co.]; Boll & Branch had about $200M and was profitable in 2023 ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/having-achieved-profitability-boll-branch-plots-retail-expansion/)) [Co., via press]; Made In grew revenue 52% from 2024 to 2025 ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/made-in-uses-its-cast-iron-launch-to-land-in-williams-sonoma/)) [Co.]. HexClad's CEO said it was on track for more than $350M in 2023 ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/everybody-kind-of-laughed-at-us-hexclad-ceo-daniel-winer/)) [Co.], which conflicts with ECDB's ~$200M estimate for 2025 [Est.]. Saatva is the strongest mattress story, bootstrapped on $350,000 and "basically profitable since day one" per its co-founder ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/?p=32353)) [Co.], but its ">$500M" revenue figure is [Unverified].

The biggest drinkware winners were not born DTC. YETI made $1.87B in FY2025, 60.4% of it direct (calc), with $165.4M of net income ([YETI](https://investors.yeti.com/news/news-details/2026/YETI-ReportsFourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Results-Provides-Full-Year-2026-Outlook/default.aspx)) [Filed]. Stanley grew from $73M in 2019 to a projected >$750M in 2023, per data CNBC reviewed ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/12/23/how-a-40-ounce-cup-turned-stanley-into-a-750-million-a-year-business.html)) [Est.]. Stanley and the DTC-born Owala share a mechanic that matters for Shelby: **limited colors of a single fixed product**, through Target-exclusive Stanley colors and Owala's "hundreds of color drops" ([Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2025/03/15/owala-water-bottle-gen-z-explosion/)). Both turned a product people rarely rebuy into a collectible.

Bulky, rarely bought products produced the category's wreckage. Casper went public at $12 a share in February 2020, was taken private at $6.90 in 2021 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/11/15/mattress-maker-casper-to-be-taken-private-by-pe-firm-durational-capital.html)), and was sold again in 2024 on undisclosed terms. Purple lost $51.4M on $468.7M of 2025 revenue ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/PRPL/purple-innovation-reports-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-fvy81ne5cu8t.html)) [Filed, via secondary], and in Q4 its ecommerce sales fell 15.3% while wholesale rose 39.8% ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/purple-innovation-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-03-31/?.tsrc=rss)) [Co.]. Solo Stove's fire-pit sales fell **43.8%** to $167.2M and its parent Solo Brands lost $145.4M ([Solo Brands](https://investors.solobrands.com/news/news-details/2026/Solo-Brands-Inc--Announces-Fiscal-2025-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-Results/default.aspx)) [Filed]. Parachute is closing 19 of 26 stores because stores opened during its expansion were "too large or in regions where brand awareness was weak" ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/parachute-close-19-stores-focus-retail-partnerships-target/751873/)). Wayfair's figures show why the category is hard: even the leader gets only **1.88 orders per customer per year** at a $312 average order [Filed], and one bulky return costs about **$55–$108** to process ([Ecommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/big-and-bulky-returns-pre-purchase-content/)) [Est., vendor].

### Food, beverage and pets: prove demand online, sell in grocery, sell to a strategic

Beverages earned the biggest disclosed checks for social-born brands. PepsiCo paid **$1.95B** for Poppi in 2025, or $1.65B net of expected tax benefits [Filed], on 2024 sales "north of $500m" [Co.]. Celsius paid **$1.8B** for Alani Nu ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1341766/000134176625000069/alaninutransactionclosin.htm)) [Filed]. Olipop doubled sales to **$400M** in 2024 and turned profitable [Co.]. Liquid Death made an estimated $333M [Est.], and Athletic Brewing passed $130M of 2024 sales ([Beer Business Daily](https://beernet.com/cbd/cbd-article/latest-athletic-profile-reveals-brewer-did-more-than-130-mill-in-sales-last-year/)) [Co.] to become the top non-alcoholic beer in US grocery, per the WSJ's analysis of NielsenIQ data ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/09/gen-z-sobriety-athletic-brewing-biggest-non-alcoholic-beer-brand-doubles-valuation/)). Huel sold to Danone for a reported ~€1bn ([NutraIngredients](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/03/23/danones-1bn-huel-deal-why-the-multinational-is-betting-big-on-meal-replacements)) [Est., FT]. Nearly all of them proved demand online, moved into natural grocery, then into mass retail and warehouse clubs, and finally sold to a strategic buyer. Olipop is in close to **50,000 grocers** ([Food Dive](https://www.fooddive.com/news/olipop-valued-at-nearly-2b-following-latest-funding-round/739907/)), Magic Spoon went from direct-only to more than 22,000 stores ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_Spoon)), and Super Coffee does about 80% of its sales in stores ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/sunniva-super-coffee/)) [Est.]. Cheap, shelf-stable drinks cannot carry the cost of shipping to individual homes, so for these brands selling direct is the launch pad, not the business.

Pets and prepared food split on whether the purchase is a necessity or a novelty. Chewy, an ecommerce-native retailer, gets **83.3%** of its $12.6B in sales from Autoship customers (up from 79.2%), who spend $591 a year each [Filed]. The Farmer's Dog sells frozen fresh food only direct, priced by dog size from about $2 a day ([The Farmer's Dog FAQ](https://www.thefarmersdog.com/faq)), and PitchBook estimates its annualized revenue reached about **$1.2B** in 2024 [Est.]. Its rivals sold well: Mars bought Nom Nom for about $1B in 2022 ([Nashville Business Journal](https://www.bizjournals.com/nashville/news/2022/01/19/mars-inc-nomnomnow-acquisition.html)) [Est., press], and Agrolimen bought Ollie for "over $600 million" in 2026 ([Pet Age](https://www.petage.com/fresh-dog-food-ollie-spain-agrolimen/)) [Est., Reuters]. Subscriptions to things people don't need did not hold. BARK's revenue fell 18.5% to $394.8M in FY2026 with a $39.0M loss ([Global Pet Industry](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/bark-prioritizes-profitability-over-subscriber-growth/)) [Filed, via press], and when it cut marketing 41.3% in one quarter, its direct revenue fell **25%** on a $31.41 average order ([BARK 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1819574/000162828026005773/exhibit991-q32026pressrele.htm)) [Filed].

Meal delivery collapsed from its pandemic peak. Blue Apron sold for about $103M in 2023 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230928876135/en)) [Filed]. Nestlé ended Freshly's home delivery two years after paying $950M for it ([Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/nestle-spins-off-freshly-into-l-catterton-venture-updates-financial-targets/)). Daily Harvest went from a $1.1B valuation [Est.] to an undisclosed sale to Chobani ([Just Food](https://www.just-food.com/news/chobani-acquires-daily-harvest/)). HelloFresh's orders fell 12.3% in 2025, yet its meal-kit margin rose to 13.5% because the business shrank down to its loyal customers ([HelloFresh](https://www.finanzen.at/nachrichten/aktien/eqs-news-fy-2025-hellofresh-se-continues-to-show-strong-aebitda-performance-while-efficiency-program-progresses-meaningfully-1035940089)) [Filed]. Prime shows hype without repeat buying: Bloomberg projected $1.2B of first-year sales [Est.], and US sales later fell **42%** year on year ([Tubefilter, citing Circana](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/22/prime-hydration-sales-decline-year-over-year-creator-product/)) [Est.].

### Electronics and wearables: Amazon-native accessories and hardware sold with a membership

The two biggest audited ecommerce-native brands sell chargers. Anker grew from an Amazon seller into a business with RMB 30.5B of 2025 revenue, and Amazon still accounted for **49.97%** of its H1 2025 revenue ([Baijing, citing the H1 report](https://www.baijing.cn/article/53904)) [Filed, via secondary]. Its own websites made up only 10.26% but grew fastest (+42.6%), and one broker note puts offline sales near 30% [Unverified]. Ugreen followed with RMB 9.49B, up 54% [Filed]. Chinese sellers now make up 49% of top sellers on Amazon.com ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/chinese-sellers-outnumber-us-sellers-on-amazoncom)) [Est.]. For Anker, diversifying has meant adding channels on top of Amazon rather than leaving it, and its 2025 recall of more than 2.38M power banks shows the risk in that model ([iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1181701403)) [Filed, via secondary].

The best-evidenced Western hardware winners sell devices worn all day and pair them with memberships. Oura's ring comes with a $5.99/month membership that rose to **20% of revenue**, while sales through Oura's own channels fell from about 73% to about 60% as retail grew ([parameter.io on the S-1](https://parameter.io/oura-oura-files-for-nasdaq-ipo-with-1-4b-revenue-and-5m-active-users/)) [Filed, via secondary]. Whoop bundles its strap into a membership, and Sacra estimates subscriptions at about **85%** of revenue ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/whoop/)) [Est.]. Eight Sleep pairs a Pod of $2,500 or more with a required monthly membership and reports reaching positive free cash flow in 2025 ([Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/unicorn-eight-sleep-funding-ai-driven-health/)) [Co.]. Owlet grew 35.4% to $105.7M ([Owlet](https://s28.q4cdn.com/849126257/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/EX-99-1-Q4-25-Earnings-Press-Release-vF.pdf)) [Filed]. Large single-purpose fitness hardware collapsed. Peloton fell from about $4.0B of revenue and a ~$49B market cap in 2021 ([Wolf Street](https://wolfstreet.com/2023/08/23/peloton-pukes-stock-sallies-to-new-record-low-97-from-peak/)) [Est., press] to $2.446B in FY2026, when paid subscribers fell **8.8%** even as price rises and cost cuts delivered its first full-year GAAP profit of $63.2M ([PeloBuddy](https://www.pelobuddy.com/peloton-q4-fy2026-earnings/)) [Filed, via secondary]. Lululemon wrote off **$442.7M** of its $500M Mirror purchase ([Yahoo Finance](https://au.finance.yahoo.com/news/lululemons-ill-timed-mirror-acquisition-is-now-almost-worthless-124822875.html)) [Filed, via secondary], and Tonal's valuation fell from $1.6B to about $550–600M ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/tonal-raises-130m-after-big-value-drop)) [Est., WSJ].

Industry gross margins come from [Damodaran's January 2026 dataset](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/margin.html); company margins come from the filings cited above, plus [e.l.f.'s FY2026 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001600033/000160003326000018/q42026er-991.htm). For apparel returns, [Loop Returns](https://info.loopreturns.com/hubfs/2024%20Winter%20Benchmark%20Report.pdf) reports 23.2% across more than 4,000 Shopify apparel merchants (a vendor figure, read via a search summary), and Coresight reports 24.4%, cited by [Red Stag](https://redstagfulfillment.com/average-return-rates-for-ecommerce/). Beauty returns come from [EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/beauty-ecommerce-margin-benchmarks), the other category ranges from [Corso](https://www.corso.com/post-purchase-resource-center/ecommerce-return-rate-benchmarks-by-industry) (a vendor that does not name its sources), and furniture from [EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/average-furniture-and-home-return-rate-benchmarks). Repeat rates come from vendor compilations by [Rivo](https://www.rivo.io/blog/shopify-customer-retention-benchmarks) and [GrowthSuite](https://www.growthsuite.net/blog/repeat-purchase-rates-by-product-category-where-retention-efforts-pay-off-most), and subscription churn from [Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/?p=27190), which covers more than 20,000 subscription brands. No independent, non-vendor dataset of return or repeat rates by category exists in this research, so read those columns as directional.

**Necessities bought on a cycle produce the most durable winners.** Chewy's Autoship customers rose from 79.2% to 83.3% of its sales [Filed], Hims's subscribers paid an average of $83 a month [Filed], Oura's membership revenue grew 121% over its latest nine months [Filed, via secondary], and fresh dog food produced three businesses or exits estimated at $600M to $1.2B [Est.]. Subscriptions to things people don't need behave differently. BARK's subscriber base shrank as soon as marketing stopped [Filed], and Prime's repeat rate is reported at about 12% ([The Robin Report](https://therobinreport.com/?p=98106)) [Unverified]. Recharge's data shows when subscriptions are won or lost: about **61% of voluntary cancellations happen at the first two renewals**, and mature churn settles at roughly 6–12% depending on category ([Recharge](https://getrecharge.com/?p=27190)) [Est.]. A subscription business is decided by its second and third orders.

**Gross margin has to pay for acquisition and absorb returns, and beauty has the most room on both.** Public beauty companies sit around 70–74% gross margin [Filed], and only 4–10% of beauty purchases come back [Est.]. Apparel's 56.9% gross margin shrinks to a 3.9% net margin at public apparel companies [Filed-derived]. About 23–25% of online apparel orders are returned [Est.], and one vendor estimate says 58% of US consumers buy several sizes and send the rest back ([Corso, citing ReadyCloud](https://www.corso.com/post-purchase-resource-center/ecommerce-return-rate-benchmarks-by-industry)) [Est.]. High gross margin also gets spent: Hims put about 39% of revenue into marketing in 2025 [Filed]. Margin pays for growth; it does not guarantee profit.

**Expensive, infrequent purchases produced the largest blowups.** Mattresses, sofas, fire pits and Peloton equipment priced at $1,695–$6,695 ([PeloBuddy](https://www.pelobuddy.com/cross-training-announcement/)) all depended on paid acquisition in categories customers buy once every few years, often after pandemic demand had been pulled forward. Parcel-shippable, giftable goods from $45 tumblers ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/12/23/how-a-40-ounce-cup-turned-stanley-into-a-750-million-a-year-business.html)) to ~$356 cookware sets ([Caraway](https://www.carawayhome.com/products/cookware-sets)) [Observed] held up better. Filed order values trace the ladder of business models: $31 at BARK, €66.8 at HelloFresh, $299 at Revolve and $312 at Wayfair per order, against $324 per customer per year at Warby Parker and $591 at Chewy [Filed]. The best economics come from a moderate first order followed by many repeats, not from one big ticket.

**Nearly every brand that scaled left pure DTC.** Warby Parker has 323 stores, Lovesac's showrooms make 67% of sales (calc), Purple's wholesale grew while its ecommerce shrank, Oura's direct share fell to about 60%, Anker still earns half its revenue on Amazon, Olipop sits in about 50,000 grocers, and True Classic's core tees are in 460 Target stores ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/how-true-classic-is-evolving-into-an-omnichannel-brand-after-hitting-500m-in-lifetime-sales/)) [Co.]. The exceptions sell what a store cannot stock: prescriptions (Hims) and frozen, personalized dog food (The Farmer's Dog). Stores do not rescue weak demand, though. Allbirds, Parachute and Casper all expanded physically on their way down. The brands that grew into retail already had a product people were buying again.

**Exits reward profit and repeat buying, and buyers who paid at the peak regretted it.** Touchland sold for about 5.4x sales and 12.7x EBITDA at its upfront price (calc), Poppi for about 3.9x 2024 sales at the gross price (calc), rhode for about 3.8x as stated by e.l.f. [Co.], Huel for about 4x [Est.] and Billie for about 3.4x (calc). Deals struck at peak buzz later soured, including Dollar Shave Club, Drunk Elephant, Kylie Cosmetics, Mirror and Freshly, and the 2017–2021 class of DTC IPOs and SPACs (Allbirds, Casper, Blue Apron, Peloton) mostly collapsed.

**Acquisition costs explain why the 2015–2020 playbook died.** Meta estimated that Apple's App Tracking Transparency would cost it about **$10B** of 2022 revenue ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/02/02/facebook-says-apple-ios-privacy-change-will-cost-10-billion-this-year.html)) [Co.], and Meta's average price per ad still rose **9–10% a year** in each of Q1–Q3 2025 ([Meta Q3 2025](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2025/Meta-Reports-Third-Quarter-2025-Results/)) [Filed]. One vendor estimates the average loss on each new customer rose from $9 in 2013 to $29 in 2022 ([SimplicityDX via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220719005425/en/Brands-Losing-a-Record-29-for-Each-New-Customer-Acquired)) [Est., vendor]. Each 2025–26 winner had a source of demand beyond paid ads: celebrity (Skims, rhode), community (Gymshark), TikTok Shop creators (Medicube), or an unusually disciplined paid-testing engine (True Classic). TikTok says sales by its US small-business sellers grew 66% in 2025, and in a survey it commissioned, 70% of its shoppers said they had bought a product a creator recommended ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/tiktok-shop-says-sales-from-u-s-small-businesses-climbed-66-in-2025/)) [Co.; survey Est.].

## Shelby should run its core tee like a consumable

Shelby is in the category with the most DTC winners by count and some of the hardest economics. Each brand it most resembles solved a different part of the problem. True Classic sold a core tee through rigorously tested paid social, reaching about $500M in lifetime sales and a run rate above $200M [Co.]. Buck Mason gets 60% of revenue from repeat customers ([Glossy](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/how-buck-mason-sold-1-million-tees-with-no-vc-funding)) [Co.]. Skims pairs a core range with constant colorways, Quince tests small batches and scales the winners, Gymshark built demand through community, and Stanley and Owala sell limited colors of one fixed product. The research found no brand that built a durable business on scarcity drops alone; Kith, the drop-cadence benchmark, does not disclose revenue ([BoF](https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/retail/where-next-for-kiths-hype-machine)). The winning pattern is an always-available core with newness layered on top.

**Fit consistency turns the core tee into a replenishment product.** A customer who has found their size can reorder without fit risk, which attacks apparel's two weakest numbers at once: returns of about 23–25% and repeat rates of about 24% [Est.]. In practice that means saving each customer's size, prompting a reorder at the tee's natural wear-out interval, and selling multipacks that raise order value without cutting the unit price. A tee "club" subscription is a reasonable test. Recharge's apparel subscriptions show the lowest first-renewal churn of any category at 16.1%, but on a thin base [Est.], so treat it as a hypothesis rather than a benchmark. Make an exchange, not a refund, the default answer to a size problem; Shelby's Medusa backend already ships order-exchange workflows (`@medusajs/core-flows`, `order/workflows/exchange`), so this is a policy and storefront change rather than new infrastructure. Price the core tee for a gross margin at or above the apparel benchmark of about 55–60% [Filed-derived], because that margin has to absorb 20–25% returns and Meta prices rising 9–10% a year.

**Drops should be variants of the core and tests that gather data, not scarcity theater.** The Stanley and Owala evidence suggests the highest-leverage drops are new colors or treatments of the proven tee silhouette. Customers already know their size in it, so each drop brings repeat orders instead of fresh fit risk. New silhouettes should run as small batches, the way Shein says it tests 100–200 units per new product and reorders what sells ([Shein](https://www.sheingroup.com/?p=4322)) [Co., not confirmed on the page] and Quince does further upmarket, with winners promoted into the core range. The playbook in [docs/playbook.md](../docs/playbook.md) already protects core availability and scores each release on contribution, sell-through and core sizes out of stock, which matches this evidence. A core stockout breaks the reorder habit and wastes the video traffic that brought the customer in. Allbirds is the warning against extending further than that: add only products the core-tee customer already wants.

**Creators and short-form video are Shelby's main way around Meta's rising prices, and TikTok Shop deserves a measured test.** A vendor that works with mid-market DTC brands reports that TikTok Shop typically reaches **5–15% of revenue** once a brand builds a program ([EightX](https://eightx.co/blog/average-ecommerce-tiktok-shop-revenue-share-by-vertical-2026)) [Est., vendor observation]. Apparel converts best there at **$5–20**, and socks, athleisure and oversized tops were strong subcategories ([FastMoss](https://www.fastmoss.com/blog/u-s-tiktok-shop-2025-sales-recap-top-products-creator-trends-fastest-growing-categories/)) [Est., vendor]. A premium core tee sits above that band, so multipacks or bundles that bring the per-unit price toward about $20, plus creator try-on videos that show fit, are the likely way to convert there (inference). Any marketplace listing has to draw from the same inventory as the Medusa store so the always-in-stock promise holds across channels. Paid Meta works only with a disciplined creative-testing system like True Classic's, and retargeting should use first-party data from the Medusa store, since App Tracking Transparency weakened third-party signals. Wholesale and owned stores can wait: mass retailers generally want brands that are "nine figures and growing" ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/operations/how-true-classic-is-evolving-into-an-omnichannel-brand-after-hitting-500m-in-lifetime-sales/)) [expert opinion]. The natural adjacent products are socks and underwear, which are replenishable, low in fit risk and strong on TikTok Shop; Bombas does more than $300M a year from socks and basics ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/?p=84667)) [Co., via press]. Whether they suit Shelby is an inference, not a finding.

**Measure the one number that decides the model.** The first-party analytics described in [docs/analytics.md](../docs/analytics.md) already capture events, funnels and experiments. The metrics that best predict whether Shelby works are the **90- and 180-day reorder rate on the core tee** by acquisition cohort and source (creator video, organic, paid), first-order contribution margin after returns, return and exchange rates by size, and revenue attributed to creators. The category benchmarks above come largely from vendors and disagree with one another; within a few months, Shelby's own cohorts will be more reliable than any of them.

## Conclusion

"DTC" was never the business model that won. The lasting winners used online channels to find a product customers buy again, then sold it wherever those customers shop, with their own website as one channel among several. What protected them was owning the relationship through a membership, an app, a saved size or Autoship, not owning the checkout. The 2025–26 class of winners (Medicube, rhode, Touchland, Poppi, Oura, Whoop, Quince) reached profitability earlier than the 2015–2020 cohort, grew through creators, marketplaces and grocery shelves rather than paid Meta, and sold to strategic buyers at 3–6x sales or raised at disciplined valuations instead of listing at peak buzz.

For Shelby, whether a drop sells out matters less than whether the people who bought the core tee buy it again. That reorder rate decides whether Shelby is an apparel brand with the economics of a consumable, or another novelty that fades once its buzz is gone.
